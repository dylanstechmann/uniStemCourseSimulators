# Ligand-field occupations, spin and spectral evidence

A donor count, formal oxidation state, spin-only moment and absorption energy are different pieces of evidence. The existing [coordination reading](04-coordination-chemistry-and-biologically-relevant-metals.md) separates these concepts, and the [ligand-balance reading](13-ligand-balance-and-coordination-models.md) works a stipulated d6 energy comparison. This reading extends the occupation bookkeeping across several electron counts and shows how an explicitly assigned photon energy can enter the limited model. All complexes and energy parameters are fictional; no observed material is identified by these calculations.

## Count electrons before assigning a spin label

For a selected transition-metal bookkeeping example, subtract the formal oxidation state from the group number to obtain the nominal d count. A group-eight center assigned oxidation state +3 is formally d5. This is an electron-counting convention, not a measurement of charge localized at the nucleus. Coordination number counts donor atoms and does not determine that d count or the number of unpaired electrons.

Adopt the elementary octahedral splitting: three lower orbitals at −0.4 Delta and two upper orbitals at +0.6 Delta. Fill them with the Pauli restriction of at most two opposite-spin electrons per orbital and the stipulated Hund filling within a degenerate set. Compare the high-spin and low-spin occupations as alternative model states. Let P be an added energy per doubly occupied orbital; it is a simplified pairing parameter, not a complete electron-correlation calculation.

## Derive occupations rather than memorize a label

For d4, high spin has three lower electrons and one upper electron, all unpaired. Its field energy is −0.6 Delta and it has no pair. Low spin has four lower electrons, one pair and two unpaired electrons; its field energy is −1.6 Delta. Thus low-minus-high total energy is −Delta+P.

For d5, high spin puts three below and two above, with five unpaired electrons, zero pairs and field energy zero. Low spin puts five below, giving two pairs, one unpaired electron and field energy −2 Delta. The energy difference is −2 Delta+2P. The existing d6 comparison has a different absolute occupation but the same difference under this simple pairing rule: high spin has four below/two above and one pair, while low spin has six below and three pairs.

For d7, high spin has five lower/two upper electrons, two pairs and three unpaired electrons, with field energy −0.8 Delta. Low spin has six lower/one upper, three pairs and one unpaired electron, with field energy −1.8 Delta. The difference is −Delta+P. Check total electrons, paired electrons and unpaired electrons in every row; a plausible energy number can conceal an impossible occupation.

These comparisons identify which of two stipulated occupations has lower model energy. They omit entropy, covalency, geometric distortions and other interactions. At equality of the simplified energies the model does not choose a unique real spin state. It supplies no measured transition temperature or universal ordering of actual complexes.

## Spin-only moments are a restricted model

With n unpaired electrons, maximal total spin S=n/2, g approximately two and orbital contributions neglected, the spin-only moment in Bohr magnetons is sqrt[n(n+2)]. For d5, the five-unpaired high-spin estimate is sqrt(35), about 5.92, while the one-unpaired low-spin estimate is sqrt(3), about 1.73. These are not counts of electrons or direct oxidation-state measurements. Real moments can include orbital and interaction effects, and mixtures or temperature dependence complicate interpretation.

A measured magnetic response alone need not identify a unique geometry, ligand set and oxidation state. Combine a model prediction with independent structural and spectroscopic evidence. Do not treat the spin-only formula as a universal inversion from one number to a full chemical identity.

## Convert an assigned transition energy carefully

For an idealized transition explicitly assigned energy Delta, a photon of wavelength lambda has energy hc/lambda. The molar energy is NA hc/lambda. Use h=6.62607015×10^−34 J s, c=299792458 m/s and NA=6.02214076×10^23 mol^−1. Converting nanometers to meters and joules to kilojoules is part of the calculation, not an optional display choice.

At an assigned wavelength of 500 nm, the molar energy is about 239.253 kJ/mol. For a separate d4 model with P=200 kJ/mol, low-minus-high energy is about −39.253 kJ/mol, favoring the low-spin candidate within this energy model. It has two unpaired electrons and spin-only moment sqrt(8). This inference depends on the stated assignment of the absorption to Delta. Real spectra can contain other transitions and selection-rule/intensity effects; a visible color name does not uniquely determine Delta.

For rehearsal, use a 600 nm assigned transition and P=210 kJ/mol in the same d4 model. Compute Delta, the energy difference, the favored occupation and its spin-only moment. Separately compare a d5 model with Delta=150 and P=180 kJ/mol. Keep those two input sets separate; a spin conclusion cannot be transferred between them simply because both are octahedral.
