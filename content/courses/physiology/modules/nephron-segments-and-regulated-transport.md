# Nephron segments and regulated transport

Clearance summarizes an output relative to a plasma concentration. It does not identify where transport occurred or separate changes in solute amount from changes in water flow. This reading connects [tubular handling](06-renal-clearance-and-tubular-handling.md) and [integrated regulation](04-renal-endocrine-and-integrated-regulation.md) to a segment-by-segment conservation ledger. It uses invented fractions to teach bookkeeping, not measured nephron transport percentages or clinical reference values.

## Connect mechanisms to separate ledgers

Tubular epithelia have different transport properties along the nephron. Basolateral sodium–potassium ATPase helps maintain the sodium electrochemical gradient that supports several apical transport processes. Proximal reabsorption recovers much of the filtered solute and water, including nutrient cotransport. Loop segments differ in water permeability and salt transport; active salt recovery in the thick ascending region and relatively water-permeable descending regions contribute to the medullary concentrating system. Countercurrent exchange in the vasa recta helps preserve that gradient, with urea also contributing to medullary osmotic organization.

Late distal and collecting segments provide regulated fine control. Aldosterone promotes sodium recovery and potassium secretion in its responsive segments. Vasopressin, also called antidiuretic hormone or ADH, increases aquaporin-mediated water permeability in collecting-duct principal cells. More permeability permits movement down an existing osmotic gradient; it does not create that gradient by itself. Renin–angiotensin signaling also couples circulatory conditions to renal and endocrine responses. These mechanisms interact; the following independent fraction changes deliberately isolate ledger consequences rather than reproduce a whole hormonal response.

The mechanism summary is supported by link-only [OpenStax A&P 2e tubular reabsorption](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-6-tubular-reabsorption) and [endocrine regulation](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/25-8-endocrine-regulation-of-kidney-function). ## Fractions apply to what arrives

Let J0 be a filtered solute amount rate. If segment i reabsorbs fraction fi of the amount delivered to that segment, with no secretion in this selected solute model, its outgoing rate is Ji=(1−fi)J(i−1). Thus final excretion is J0 times the product of all retained fractions. Adding the fi values would be wrong because their denominators differ. A fraction of delivered load is not the same as a fraction of original filtered load.

Use a separate water-flow ledger with segment fractions wi. Urine volume flow is the initial filtrate flow times the product of (1−wi). Final urine concentration is final solute amount rate divided by final water volume rate, with compatible units. A high or low concentration alone does not tell you whether the excreted amount rose or fell. Additional secretion would enter as a distinct amount term at the relevant segment, not a negative water fraction.

## Worked synthetic sequence

Stipulate filtration flow 100 mL/min and plasma sodium concentration 140 mmol/L. The filtered sodium load is 0.100×140=14 mmol/min. Invent four successive sodium reabsorption fractions 0.6, 0.75, 0.8 and 0.9. Delivered rates after each stage are 5.6, 1.4, 0.28 and 0.028 mmol/min. Fractional excretion is 0.028/14=0.002, or 0.2 percent. Check each removed amount plus its outgoing amount returns that stage's incoming amount.

For a separate water ledger in the same toy sequence, use fractions 0.6, 0.5, zero and 0.95. Volume flows become 40, 20, 20 and 1 mL/min. Final sodium concentration is 0.028 mmol/min divided by 0.001 L/min, or 28 mmol/L. These input fractions are mathematical choices; they are not a complete validated anatomical or hormonal model. In particular, assigning a zero water fraction to one aggregated stage is an explicit simplification.

## Water and sodium perturbations can look different

Change only final water recovery from 0.95 to 0.5 while retaining the stipulated sodium fractions. Final water flow becomes 10 mL/min and sodium concentration 2.8 mmol/L, while sodium excretion remains 0.028 mmol/min. A tenfold concentration decrease here reflects water dilution, not a tenfold reduction in sodium loss. Conversely, changing final sodium recovery from 0.9 to 0.8 doubles sodium excretion at a fixed water ledger. The toy independence makes the distinction transparent; a real endocrine perturbation can alter several processes together.

Clearance adds another ratio: sodium clearance equals excretion divided by plasma concentration. In the original example, 0.028/140 L/min is 0.0002 L/min, or 0.2 mL/min. Its ratio to filtration flow is the same fractional excretion 0.002 under the freely filtered/no-other-source assumptions. Do not interpret a clearance volume as the actual urine volume or as a segment's anatomical flow.

## Distinct rehearsal and state balance

For rehearsal, use filtration 80 mL/min, plasma sodium 125 mmol/L, sodium fractions 0.5, 0.8, 0.75, 0.9 and water fractions 0.5, 0.5, zero, 0.96. Derive filtered load, final excretion, fractional excretion, urine flow and urine concentration. Then change only the final water fraction to 0.8. Compare amount and concentration explicitly before describing a response.

A body-pool balance must also include intake and other losses: dN/dt=amount input−renal excretion−other output. Steady sodium concentration does not by itself establish unchanged sodium amount because the water volume can change too. To model concentration N/V, differentiate both numerator and denominator. Hormonal feedback and reserve determine how the flows respond over time; the [feedback reading](delayed-feedback-and-water-balance.md) develops one limited normalized example.
