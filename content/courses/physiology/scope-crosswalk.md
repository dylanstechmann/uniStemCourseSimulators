# Human Physiology for Engineers scope crosswalk

This crosswalk compares the local package, version 0.7.0, with three published physiology courses: MIT OpenCourseWare HST.542J Quantitative Physiology: Organ Transport Systems, Spring 2004 ([calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-542j-quantitative-physiology-organ-transport-systems-spring-2004/pages/calendar/), 25 lectures and six labs; cable and Hodgkin–Huxley scope also from [6.021J, Fall 2004](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-021j-quantitative-physiology-cells-and-tissues-fall-2004/pages/calendar/)); the University of Arizona PSIO/BME 511 Physiology for Biomedical Engineering, Spring 2023 ([syllabus](https://www.physiology.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2023-09/BME_PSIO%20511%20Syllabus%202023.pdf), six systems sections); and the University of Colorado Boulder MCEN 4117/5117 Quantitative Physiology for Engineers ([syllabus](https://www.colorado.edu/lab/tan/sites/default/files/attached-files/mcen4171.pdf), a spring-term schedule whose year is not printed). It was compiled with AI assistance on 2026-10-30 from lecture and unit titles and the local readings and practice. It is an agent-run comparison, not a review by the owner or by a physiology instructor. It does not compare lecture content beyond those titles, problem sets, exams or workload, and topic alignment establishes no equivalence, credit or endorsement.

Two scope notes apply. Arizona PSIO/BME 511 is a systems-level medical-physiology course for biomedical engineers that also teaches neurophysiology, gastrointestinal and reproductive physiology, so it widens the topic list well beyond this package. Colorado MCEN 4117/5117 and MIT HST.542J are the closer quantitative comparators. Status labels: **covered** means a local reading develops the topic with a worked example and practice; **shallow** means a local reading teaches part of the topic; **absent** means no local instruction; **elsewhere** means another local course teaches it; **local extension** means the package teaches a topic no comparator names. Local numbers are lesson IDs (physiology-N), and each links to its reading.

## What 0.7.0 changed

The second-university comparison found two topics that both Arizona and Colorado teach and that the package lacked. The earlier audit row for this course recorded the heart as a pump but not electrocardiography, which MIT HST.542J itself gives three lectures. A new week-5 reading, lesson 25, adds time to the pressure–volume loop: valve logic assigns the four phases, a stipulated filling model shows why diastole absorbs the shortening of the cycle and why cardiac output can peak and then fall with rate, conduction delays follow from distance and velocity, a pacemaker rate follows from the slope of diastolic depolarization, and the limb leads are treated as projections of a cardiac vector with Einthoven's law. A new week-7 reading, lesson 24, develops the Henderson–Hasselbalch relation as a ratio of two regulated terms, classifies disturbances, quantifies compensation with the loop gain of the feedback lesson, treats the kidney as a ledger of filtered load and daily acid excretion, reads the anion gap as electroneutrality bookkeeping and applies the Fick balance to CO₂. Together they add eight objectives, 24 practice items and nine retrieval cards.

## Remaining gaps

- Baroreflex, autoregulation and local control of organ blood flow (Arizona; MIT Lec 8–9).
- Endocrine axes: glucose homeostasis, thyroid, calcium and adrenal physiology (Arizona).
- Neurophysiology beyond the membrane, gastrointestinal physiology and reproduction (Arizona).
- Smooth muscle and the neuromuscular junction (Colorado Unit 3c).
- Arrhythmias and clinical electrocardiography (MIT Lec 7), carbamino chemistry and the chloride shift, and diffusion-limited gas transfer.
- Reading a primary physiology article, graded homework and exams, measured workload, and review by a qualified person.
