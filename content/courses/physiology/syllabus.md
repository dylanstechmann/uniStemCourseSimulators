# Human Physiology for Engineers

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.6.0.**

Quantitative homeostasis across excitable, cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, and endocrine systems, with emphasis on feedback and transport.

## Prerequisites

Cell biology; Physics: Mechanics; calculus recommended. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Use mass and energy balance to describe homeostasis.
- Interpret pressure-flow and resistance relationships.
- Connect membrane transport to organ-level function.
- Predict responses to controlled perturbations.

## Proposed 14-week schedule

This is a proposed scope, not a measured or reviewed semester. The original homeostasis and excitable-cell prototypes remain concise primers; the cardiovascular/respiratory and renal prototypes have longer explanations. Separate passive cable and Hodgkin–Huxley readings develop membrane dynamics. The new nephron, delayed-feedback and parallel gas-exchange foundations connect to four open coursework reports. ## Assessment and study policy

Current practice inventory: 25 lessons, 139 public practice items, 89 retrieval cards and 6 Case studio prompts, including five project milestones. Counts do not measure semester depth or independently reviewed quality.

Every lesson has public formative items (numeric, single-choice, multiple-select, data-interpretation, structured-response and CSV-upload) mapped to its objectives, plus retrieval cards for the review queue; each practice assessment also lists the course outcomes its items assess. Week 13 is a synthetic-data virtual lab and week 14 pairs a compensation-and-reserve lesson with the course case and its self-assessment checklist. These are practice activities with unlimited retries and no institutional grade, university credit or transferable credit. An [inactive, ungraded renal-clearance ledger draft](assessment-specs/renal-clearance-ledger-project-draft.md) is provided for instructor review and is not activated in the course platform. Workload has not been measured.

Four original reports require passive/gated membrane and cable checks (week 4), parallel resistance/content/delivery and mechanism evidence (week 10), a serial nephron amount/water ledger with separate perturbations (week 12), and delayed feedback, bounded control and sensor bias (week 14). Submit selected numeric/interpretation checkpoints in the browser; retain solver code, derivations, plots, convergence evidence and reports locally. The software does not grade those artifacts. The existing synthetic scratch-assay lab and renal-clearance project remain available.

## Current limitations

The schedule above is proposed, not measured, and has not been reviewed by a qualified instructor. Nephron removal fractions, scaled feedback parameters and the nonlinear oxygen-binding curve are invented teaching models rather than measured human estimates. Equivalent bypass fractions do not identify an anatomical mechanism; negative controller states denote deviations below a reference. The existing virtual lab does not establish experimental laboratory competence. There are no graded homework sets, midterm, final or active graded project; the renal-clearance ledger is an inactive authoring draft. The cardiac pressure-volume example is a rectangular approximation using invented values and is not a clinical estimate. The Hodgkin–Huxley reading links the 1952 paper; its classic parameters were not checked against the scanned original. Other readings teach standard relations with synthetic inputs, and statements about average changes with age remain general textbook-level statements without cited sources. All scenarios and people are synthetic; no lesson gives medical advice, and nothing here says anything about any person's health. Qualified human review is required before this course could meet the complete-course standard.
