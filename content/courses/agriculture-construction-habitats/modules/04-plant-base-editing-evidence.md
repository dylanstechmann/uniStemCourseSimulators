# Plant base-editing evidence: controls, comparators and limitations

**Status:** Original evidence-literacy lesson in partial version 0.1.0; unreviewed and formative-only. It contains no guide/target design, delivery or regeneration procedure, sequence-level exercise or other operational plant-editing protocol. All counts below are synthetic.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish molecular editing outcomes from trait, agronomic and whole-system outcomes using appropriate comparators.
2. Interpret synthetic treatment and control counts while preserving independent-lineage, background-variant and assay limitations.
3. Use the Zong correction and Jin off-target study to evaluate evidence provenance, uncertainty and remaining review needs.

## One component in a larger system

Plant DNA base editing is only one component of this survey. At a high level, a cytosine base editor can produce a specified class of single-base molecular outcome without requiring the same repair logic as a double-strand-break approach. That statement does not establish what happened in any new experiment. Evidence must identify the biological material, molecular assay, comparators, independent lineages and endpoints actually assessed.

Claims sit at different levels. A sequencing result addresses a molecular endpoint. A visible or measured phenotype addresses an organismal endpoint. Yield, quality and resource use address agronomic endpoints. Energy demand, runoff, waste and resilience address system endpoints. A favourable result at one level cannot be promoted automatically to the next. The plant trait may require environmental context, and the system result may be dominated by lighting, climate or handling rather than genotype.

## Comparators and the alternatives they test

A matched untreated or process comparator can help separate a treatment-associated result from handling and background variation. A molecular assay comparator can address detection artefacts. Independent biological lineages help test whether an outcome is peculiar to one lineage. Each comparator answers only some alternatives; none makes the design self-proving.

**Synthetic molecular example.** Eighteen of 72 assessed plants show a declared molecular outcome, so 18/72 × 100 = **25%**. This is an outcome frequency in an invented table. It does not establish editing mechanism, uniformity among cells, absence of unintended variants or agronomic value.

**Synthetic phenotype example.** A treated group shows a phenotype in 14/60 plants and its comparator in 6/60. The treated-minus-comparator difference is (14/60 − 6/60) × 100 = **13.333333… percentage points**. Equal denominators simplify the arithmetic but do not guarantee independence or eliminate confounding. The exercise should state how lineages arose, whether observations are independent and whether assessors knew group assignment.

**Synthetic variant example.** A sequencing summary calls 10 variants in a treated lineage and 4 background variants in a matched control. The arithmetic excess is **6 variants**. Their cause and functional significance remain unresolved without lineage history, assay performance and broader genome-wide evaluation.

## Provenance includes corrections and critical studies

Zong et al. reported plant cytosine base editing in 2017. The evidence record must include the [original article, DOI 10.1038/nbt.3811, PMID 28244994](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.3811) and the [2025 author correction, DOI 10.1038/s41587-025-02671-3, PMID 40275090](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-025-02671-3). The correction is a distinct publication record connected to the article, not an independent replication and not a reason to pretend the scholarly record never changed.

Jin et al. examined unintended genome-wide variants in rice and provides a separate critical comparison: [DOI 10.1126/science.aaw7166, PMID 30819931](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw7166). Its title is not a universal rule for every editor, species or design. It motivates careful comparator selection and genome-wide specificity review rather than a slogan that one class is always safe or unsafe.

A review memo should distinguish what each publication directly supports, what this lesson infers at a high level and what remains unknown. It should record the version consulted and never substitute identifiers from an unrelated secondary repository. The registry links above are the authoritative citation anchors for this package; no paper text, figure, table or dataset is reproduced.

## System-context questions

Even a well-supported molecular and phenotypic result would leave system questions. Does the phenotype persist across relevant environments? Are lineages independently reproduced? Do unintended variants or background differences matter? Does the trait alter input demand, product quality or waste? Which effects come from the production environment rather than genetics? These require new evidence, not extrapolation from the molecular percentage.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a molecular outcome as proof of agronomic or resource-system benefit.
- Using a comparator as a substitute for independent lineages or for genome-wide specificity assessment.
- Reporting percentage differences as percentages rather than percentage points.
- Citing the Zong article without its 2025 correction or treating Jin as that correction.
- Generalizing an off-target finding beyond the evidence while ignoring the need for specificity review.
