# Electrical Circuits & Instrumentation

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.8.0.** Personal hobby and learning project with substantial AI assistance; no university equivalence or institutional credit.

Original instruction develops nodal/port balances, finite feedback, differential/common-mode errors, filter loading/recovery, noise and uncertainty, acquisition and calibration domains, second-order RLC transients and frequency response, op-amp summing, integrating, differentiating and active filtering, alternating-current power with root-mean-square bookkeeping and cycle-by-cycle energy accounting, and the declared digital abstraction with Boolean gate routes, memory elements and timing discipline. A cumulative final channel audit closes the declared scope. An original text feedback map includes a verbal description. Preserved sensor-loading/ADC and first-order frequency-response lessons remain.

## Prerequisites

Physics: Electricity & Magnetism; Calculus I recommended. Manifest relationships remain provisional and establish no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Solve DC networks with Kirchhoff and Thevenin methods.
- Analyze first- and second-order transient and sinusoidal frequency response.
- Select an amplifier topology for a sensor signal.
- Quantify noise, loading, and calibration limits.

## Optional and beyond the scope of this course

A broader strand was considered for this package and deliberately kept out: energy-aware digital hardware. Gate-level CMOS construction, digital memory arrays, and the static and switching energy of logic are the natural next depth for a circuits course — the energy per logical operation has become a first-order constraint in the AI-era accelerator world, which is what made the temptation timely — but none of it serves the sensor-and-acquisition mission this narrower instrumentation package declares. It is recorded here as an optional pointer, not as taught, assessed or claimed content: the [MIT OCW 6.002 Circuits and Electronics, Spring 2007 calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/calendar/) schedules MOS gate design in L5, static power in digital circuits in recitation R6, digital memory arrays in R15 and CMOS energy and power in R24, with the assigned Agarwal and Lang textbook chapters (6.1–6.8, 4.1–4.3, 10.6.3–10.6.4 and 11.5) listed on the public [readings page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/readings/). A reader wanting that strand should take it directly from those sources, and its absence here is a boundary statement, not a completeness claim about them.
