# Signals, systems and feedback-control scope

This is a partial, application-focused undergraduate sequence at the level of introductory linear dynamic systems and single-input/single-output feedback. Its title reflects two strands taught together; it does not claim to replace either comparator course or cover their full scope.

## Where the strands join

The course starts from continuous-time plant models, time and frequency responses, then uses feedback stability and margins to reason about controller choice. Sampling and anti-alias filtering lead into a first-order discrete recurrence and proportional closed-loop pole; the sampled PI lesson and filter-retuning case apply that representation to controller behavior. This is a selected bridge between the two comparators, not a claim of complete discrete-time control design.

The scope crosswalk is a maintainer and learner reference. Existing lesson IDs, assessments and counts are unchanged. All examples and data remain synthetic; the course provides no real-device settings, safety procedure or implementation protocol. Workload, qualified instruction review and accessibility review remain open. The comparison is link-only; no MIT text, figures, data or assessments were reused.
