# Calculus II: Integration, Series & Applications

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.7.0.** This personal hobby and learning project is developed with substantial AI assistance and is not an accredited university course.

Integration and infinite series describe accumulated quantities and controlled approximations. Synthetic examples connect mathematical structure with rates, work, geometric area, radial amounts, averages and probability densities. See the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) for the comparison with MIT OCW 18.01SC and Harvard Math 1b.

## Prerequisites

Calculus I, including derivatives, definite integrals, substitution and elementary functions. Manifest prerequisites are provisional authoring associations and establish no university equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Select and justify integration methods and convergence of improper integrals.
- Set up integrals for work, area, and volume.
- Test convergence of standard series.
- Use Taylor approximations with an error interpretation.
- Evaluate selected indeterminate limits and justify the method used.

Numeric, single-choice, CSV-upload, structured-choice and multiple-select items provide selected feedback with unlimited retries. Points are practice feedback, with no institutional grade or credit. Assessment outcome lists reflect the lesson objectives assessed by their items; prototype associations remain provisional.

## Connected coursework (open, ungraded)

Four original mixed reports require method/domain and rational-tail evidence (week 4), frustum/coverage and radial-quantile calculations (week 6), convergence hypotheses and exact integer certificates (week 9), and integrated arctangent approximation/precision plus interchange/representation counterexamples (week 13). Two foundations in weeks 5 and 11 develop surface elements/multiplicity and selected function-limit/Taylor conditions. The arctangent approximation field requests six significant figures to distinguish it from the reference; other new continuous fields use three. Retain diagrams, code and proofs locally; browser checkpoints evaluate selected values and choices. The existing force lab and tail project remain available. Counts do not establish semester depth or measured workload.
