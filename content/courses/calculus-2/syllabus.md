# Calculus II: Integration, Series & Applications

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.4.8.** This personal hobby and learning project is developed with substantial AI assistance and is not an accredited university course.

Integration and infinite series describe accumulated quantities and controlled approximations. Synthetic examples connect mathematical structure with rates, work, geometric area and radial amounts.

## Prerequisites

Calculus I, including derivatives, definite integrals, substitution and elementary functions. Manifest prerequisites are provisional authoring associations and establish no university equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Select and justify integration methods and convergence of improper integrals.
- Set up integrals for work, area, and volume.
- Test convergence of standard series.
- Use Taylor approximations with an error interpretation.
- Evaluate selected indeterminate limits and justify the method used.

## Proposed 14-week schedule

This sequence is planning scope, not measured workload or a reviewed semester. Week 2 pairs integration-method selection with trigonometric powers, trigonometric substitution and completing the square. Week 3 pairs selected indeterminate limits with improper-integral convergence. Weeks 1, 4, 7 and 10 also have expanded readings on integration methods, force work, signed accumulation, series convergence and Taylor error. Week 5 prepares the preserved radial-concentration case, revisited in week 14 alongside the synthetic force-work lab. All activities are public formative practice.

## Current limitations

Selected indeterminate limits and standard trigonometric substitutions are now included, but coverage is not exhaustive. One synthetic lab is not a laboratory sequence. There is no graded homework sequence, midterm, final or activated cumulative project; the new draft is inactive. Workload, accessibility and mathematical quality await qualified review. Mathematical bounds apply only under their stated conditions and control neither measurement uncertainty nor model discrepancy. All force and density examples are constructed and establish no device specification or tissue behavior. The course remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only.
