# Calculus I scope crosswalk

This crosswalk compares the local package, version 0.7.0, with two openly published undergraduate single-variable calculus courses: MIT OpenCourseWare 18.01SC Single Variable Calculus, Fall 2010 ([syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/), Units 1–3), and Harvard Math 1a Introduction to Calculus, Spring 2005 ([syllabus](https://abel.math.harvard.edu/archive/1a_spring_05/syllabus.html) and [calendar](https://abel.math.harvard.edu/archive/1a_spring_05/calendar_mwf.html)). It was compiled with AI assistance on 2026-10-28 from each comparator's public unit, part, session and class-meeting titles and from the local readings and practice. It is an agent-run comparison, not a review by the owner or by a mathematics instructor. It does not compare lecture content, problem sets, exams or workload, and topic alignment establishes no equivalence, credit or endorsement.

Two scope notes apply. MIT 18.01SC covers all of single-variable calculus, and the local package splits that sequence between Calculus I and Calculus II, so only 18.01SC's differentiation, applications-of-differentiation and definite-integral units (Units 1 through 3) bear on this package; Units 4 and 5 belong to Calculus II. Harvard's calendar lists a textbook section for each meeting, and its first three meetings (welcome and precalculus functions) are prerequisite review. MIT's session titles were read for Unit 1 Parts A and B and Unit 3 Parts B and C, and the other rows compare at part level. Status labels: **covered** means a local reading develops the topic with a worked example and practice; **absent** means no local instruction; **elsewhere** means another local course teaches it. Local numbers are lesson IDs (calculus-1-N), and each links to its reading.

## What 0.7.0 changed

The package differentiated products, quotients, composites, inverse functions, exponentials and logarithms, but not the trigonometric functions: the only mention of sine and cosine was in the inverse-trigonometric section. Both comparators teach this strand, 18.01SC in Unit 1 Part A (sessions 7 and 8) and Harvard in meeting 3.4. A new week-3 reading follows the derivative-rules lesson. It proves the two limits of (sin h)/h and (cos h − 1)/h with the squeeze theorem, derives the derivatives of sine, cosine and tangent, and applies them to a sinusoidal signal: its largest slope, its zero-slope points, the relation x″ = −ω²x, the factor that radians introduce, and the small-angle approximation. It adds three objectives, 13 practice items and four retrieval cards.
