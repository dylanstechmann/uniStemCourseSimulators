# Calculus I: Differential and Integral Calculus

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.7.0.** This personal hobby and learning project is developed with substantial AI assistance. It is not an accredited university course.

Limits, derivatives and integrals describe local change and accumulation, with constructed examples in growth, transport, geometry and measurement. See the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) for the comparison with MIT OCW 18.01SC and Harvard Math 1a.

## Prerequisites

Algebra, functions and trigonometry. Structured prerequisite relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Evaluate limits and justify continuity claims.
- Differentiate composite and implicit functions.
- Use derivatives to analyze and optimize models.
- Interpret definite integrals as accumulated change.
- Apply Rolle's and Mean Value Theorems and their consequences to justify monotonicity, uniqueness, and rate bounds.

## Connected mixed coursework (open, ungraded)

Four assignments in weeks 3, 6, 9 and 14 require locally retained limit/proof/counterexample arguments, implicit/explicit and inverse/related-rate cross-checks, constrained optimization and MVT/root certificates, and cumulative signed-accumulation/quadrature verification. A new week-10 foundation derives finite sums, proves a local FTC implication and tests piecewise-rate hypotheses. Browser checkpoints assess selected results, not proofs, diagrams, implementations or reports. No measured workload or qualified approval is inferred.

## Current limitations

No compact prototype reading remains. There is no graded homework sequence, midterm, final or activated integrative project; the optional project guide and its rehearsal are ungraded practice, and the washout portfolio draft remains inactive. One synthetic lab is not a laboratory sequence. Workload, accessibility and content have not been reviewed by a qualified person. Numerical bounds require the stated smoothness, domains and noise assumptions. All washout signals are constructed; the course gives no real clearance estimates, experimental protocols or device specifications. MIT 18.01SC Units 4 and 5 belong to the Calculus II course, L'Hôpital's rule and parametric curves are taught there, and differential equations are taught in the Differential Equations course. The package remains partial and formative-only.
