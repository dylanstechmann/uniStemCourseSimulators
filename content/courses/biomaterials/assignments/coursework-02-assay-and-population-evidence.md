## Task A: define every denominator

Calculate the initial attached fraction, the final count relative to delivered cells and the later population fold change relative to attached cells. The attached fraction is 0.5, final count divided by delivered cells is one, and attached-population fold change is two. These values are mutually consistent. Calling the final state “no growth” because it equals the number delivered would erase the initial attachment loss and use the wrong reference for the later trajectory.

Fit the effective constant net rate r=ln2/3 per day using the two attached-population counts. Write the resulting N(t) and predict the day-six count under that same model. Mark it as an extrapolation. Explain why an exponential fitted to two endpoints does not establish indefinite proliferation, carrying capacity or the absence of a lag. Sampling a different visible depth at day three would also violate the compatible-count assumption.

## Task B: reconstruct the signal model

A separate reporter-spike comparison has recovery 0.75 on the material and one in the reference vessel. Under the stipulated equal linear-recovery assumption, the inverse-recovery factor is 4/3. ## Task C: expose nonidentifiability of division and loss

Use dN/dt=(g−d)N. Construct one model with g=0.4 per day and d=0.4−ln2/3, and another with g=0.6 and d=0.6−ln2/3. Check that both loss rates are positive and both reproduce every point on the same fitted net trajectory. Additional time points of exactly that ideal count curve identify r more precisely but cannot separate g from d because only their difference enters the equation.

Specify a separate division or loss observable that could distinguish the models. Include a plausible limitation of that observable rather than treating its label as guaranteed truth. For example, a tracked loss compartment may miss detached cells that disintegrate, and an imaging-based division count may depend on visibility. The task is to make the missing information explicit, not to claim that one generic control solves every case.
