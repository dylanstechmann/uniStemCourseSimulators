# Biomaterials & Tissue Engineering

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.5.0.**

This partial, application-focused undergraduate course develops core biomaterials and cell-material reasoning through material properties, protein adsorption, scaffold transport/degradation, host-response evidence and failure. Tissue engineering is a selected application thread in weeks 9–14, not comprehensive training in regeneration mechanisms, scaffold fabrication, clinical translation or implant design.

See the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) for comparison with undergraduate MIT OCW 3.051J and the selective graduate 20.441J comparator. The title is retained because tissue engineering is a stated application; the mapped omissions define its boundary.

## Prerequisites

Cell biology; Organic Chemistry I; Statics/Materials; Transport recommended. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Relate material chemistry and architecture to biological response.
- Estimate scaffold transport and mechanical constraints.
- Compare scaffold, cell, and signal strategies for a tissue target.
- Interpret in-vitro and in-vivo evidence and identify application-specific failure modes.

## Proposed 14-week schedule

This is a proposed scope, not a measured or reviewed semester. Week 1 links the material-properties primer to a comparison of material classes, surface-characterization methods, protein adsorption and application-specific host-response evidence. Weeks 3, 9 and 13 also have expanded scaffold, cell/signal and translation/failure-analysis primers, and week 6 adds a bounded controlled-release kinetics reading. The four original prototype readings remain short primers despite this expansion. Every lesson has public formative items and retrieval cards.

## Assessment and study policy

Every lesson has public formative items (numeric, single-choice, multiple-select, data-interpretation, structured-response and CSV-upload) mapped to its objectives, plus retrieval cards for the review queue; each practice assessment also lists the course outcomes its items assess. Week 1 uses explicitly synthetic coupon data to practice comparing material classes and interpreting surface and host-response measurements. Week 12 is a synthetic-data virtual lab with an optional scaffold-property project guide and rehearsal practice, and week 14 pairs a lesson on reading a failure in a degrading vascular scaffold with the course case and its self-assessment checklist. These are practice activities with unlimited retries and no institutional grade, university credit or transferable credit. Workload has not been measured.

## Connected coursework (open, ungraded)

Two original foundations add accessible surface-grafting/competing-layer mechanisms (week 2) and quantitative cell-signal/attachment/net-growth models (week 5). Three reports require interface-accessibility evidence (week 6), count/assay/function evidence (week 10), and a cumulative architecture/interface/construct dossier (week 14). Fourteen checkpoints/cards assess selected values and interpretations; complete reports, plots, code and experiments remain locally retained and ungraded. Current inventory is 22 routes, 122 practice items and 73 cards. Scheduling and counts do not establish measured semester workload.

## Optional scaffold-property project (ungraded)

The [scaffold project guide](assignments/scaffold-property-project-guide.md) makes the project below usable as open practice. It restates the project's candidates, criteria and tasks, works a separate synthetic candidate, and pairs two rehearsal candidates with open practice items in week 12. Five milestones, suggested for weeks 12–13, are Case studio self-checks whose responses are saved privately and never submitted, collected or scored.

## Inactive integrative project draft

The [scaffold-property portfolio](assessment-specs/scaffold-property-project-draft.md) compares synthetic porosity, permeability and modulus trajectories against explicit teaching thresholds at two time horizons. It extends the degradation lab into a multi-constraint evidence exercise, not a material-selection recommendation. The draft itself is not scheduled, assigned, submitted or graded, and the open project guide above does not submit work to it; review of assumptions, workload and accessibility remains open.

## Current limitations

The schedule above is proposed, not measured, and has not been reviewed by a qualified instructor. There are no graded homework sets, midterm or final; the optional project guide and its rehearsal are ungraded practice, and the project draft remains inactive. The package covers selected tissue-engineering applications but only selected surface modification, quantitative cell-assay and controlled-release mechanisms rather than the undergraduate comparator's broader coating/corrosion, drug-delivery, biosensor, organ-replacement or regulatory topics, or the graduate comparator's detailed wound-healing, ECM and tissue-regeneration sequence. All numerical coupon data, materials, cells, animals and other values are synthetic; no lesson gives a laboratory protocol or medical advice, and nothing here says anything about any real material, device, cell line or person. Qualified human review is required before this course could meet the complete-course standard.
