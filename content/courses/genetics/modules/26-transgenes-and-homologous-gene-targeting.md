# Transgenes and homologous gene targeting

Mutagenesis is not the only way to change a locus: a designed construct can replace it through homologous recombination, and embryonic-stem-cell chimera work can carry that replacement into an organism. This original reading adds targeting-construct design, double selection and the chimera route to the existing recombination and mapping material. Every construct, cell count and percentage here is an invented teaching parameter, not a protocol, an animal-work authorization or a validated procedure.

## A targeting construct is two arms and a marker

Stipulate a targeting vector: a left homology arm of 5.0 kb, a right arm of 3.0 kb, and between them a 2.0 kb marker that replaces a 1.5 kb stretch containing the gene's second exon. The homology totals 8.0 kb, and a replacement — as opposed to an insertion — needs a double crossover, one exchange in each arm, so exactly two exchange events are required. The replaced locus comes out 0.5 kb longer than wild type: the marker contributes 2.0 kb where 1.5 kb of coding sequence used to be. Net length change is a design fact, checkable on a restriction map before any cell is touched, and the audit habit of this package applies: write down the predicted structure first, then look at data.

## Positive and negative selection

Stipulate two times ten million electroporated stem cells. The homologous integration frequency is 1.0×10⁻⁶, so about 20 targeted colonies are expected. Random integration is far more generous, 1.0×10⁻⁴, or about 2000 colonies, one hundred times the targeted number. The construct therefore carries a positive marker — conferring resistance — inside the arms, and a negative marker outside them, an enzyme that converts a supplied drug into a toxin only when the construct integrates randomly and keeps its outsides. Stipulate that the negative selection kills 99 percent of random integrants: about 20 random survivors remain, alongside the 20 targeted colonies, so the double-resistant pool is 40 colonies and half of it is correctly targeted. The enrichment is large but imperfect — 50 percent, not 100 percent — so verifying each colony by DNA analysis is not optional bookkeeping. Selection narrows a pool; it does not identify a clone.

## From chimera to germline

Targeted stem cells are introduced into an early embryo of a distinguishable coat-color genotype; the resulting animal is a chimera whose coat mosaicism reports the contribution. Stipulate a chimera with 40 percent donor contribution: if those cells have entered the germline, a testcross expects about 40 percent donor-colored offspring, and coat color is the cheap proxy for genotype here. Crossing a confirmed heterozygous carrier to wild type yields carriers; the informative cross is heterozygote to heterozygote, yielding the Mendelian 1:2:1 — in stipulated percentages, 25.0 homozygous replacement, 50.0 heterozygous carrier, 25.0 wild type. The replacement allele is now a strain property, tracked by ordinary Mendelian bookkeeping, and the sex-chromosome and pedigree rules from earlier lessons apply unchanged.

## Conditional targeting with loxP

A null allele in every cell is often too blunt. The standard refinement records two loxP sites per allele flanking a chosen exon — here exon 4 — so the allele is floxed: wild type in function, marked for deletion. In a homozygous floxed cell the two alleles carry four loxP sites in total. Cre recombinase expressed from a tissue-specific promoter excises the flanked exon only where it is active, so the knockout appears in that tissue and not elsewhere, and the same animal carries both genotypes in different organs. The design decisions — exon choice, promoter choice, timing — determine what the experiment can claim, and the audit habit applies again: state the predicted allele structure per tissue before looking at any assay.

## Common mistakes

Do not treat double selection as proof of targeting: 50 percent enrichment still leaves half the pool to verify. Do not call a floxed allele a knockout: without Cre it is functionally wild type. Do not confuse coat mosaicism with germline transmission: somatic contribution and heritable contribution are different facts about the same animal. Do not add the marker's length to both sides of the ledger: only the replaced stretch leaves.

## Limits of this lesson

Every frequency, cell count and percentage is synthetic. Nothing here describes a real cell line, authorizes embryo work, or predicts any experiment's yield. Qualified review and biological validation remain outside this package.
