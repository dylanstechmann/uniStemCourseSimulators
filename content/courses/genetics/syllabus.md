# Genetics & Genomics

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.7.0.**

Move from Mendelian inheritance and recombination to microbial genetics, gene regulation and experimental inference. Advanced human causal-inference applications are optional extensions.

## Prerequisites

Foundations of Cell & Molecular Biology. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Calculate genotype probabilities under explicit assumptions.
- Interpret linkage and recombination data.
- Distinguish association from causal variant evidence.
- Design a controlled gene-expression experiment.
- Analyze microbial genetic-transfer and complementation evidence.
- Predict prokaryotic gene expression from regulatory genotypes and environmental inputs.
- Analyze one-locus selection/mutation and genotype-association models and distinguish linkage likelihood from causal probability.

## Assessment and study policy

Every lesson has public formative items mapped to its objectives and retrieval cards for the review queue; practice assessments list linked course outcomes. Week 13 is a synthetic-data virtual lab. Week 14 combines a population/linkage report with two optional human-genetics applications and the course case checklist. Weeks 10 and 13 add functional-genetics and genome-evidence reports. An optional three-point mapping project guide in week 6 adds an open rehearsal practice set and five Case studio milestone self-checks; milestone responses can be saved privately and are not submitted for grading. These activities are ungraded practice with unlimited retries and provide no institutional or transferable credit. Workload has not been measured.

## Connected evidence coursework (open, ungraded)

Three new assignments require a functional-genetics matrix/haplotype/operon report (week 10), a copy/transcript/protein evidence dossier using an original clone-count table (week 13), and a tested one-locus population simulation plus separate linkage likelihood analysis (week 14). Each supplies distinct inputs, verification tasks and self-review values. Browser items evaluate selected responses only; written mechanisms, causal diagrams, implementations and reports need actual human feedback. Their workload is unmeasured.

## Current limitations

The schedule above is proposed, not measured, and has not been reviewed by a qualified instructor. All compact prototype readings now have expanded instruction. There are no graded homework sets, midterm or final; the optional project guide and its rehearsal are ungraded practice, and the project draft remains inactive. The course now includes focused bacterial-transfer, operon-circuit and tetrad instruction (unordered two-locus classes and single-exchange centromere distance from ordered asci), with new phage functional-map/suppression, eukaryotic dosage/transcript and population/linkage instruction and three connected assignments. Broader tetrad genetics, transposition, phage life cycles, genome targeting, cancer genetics and multi-locus/pedigree topics remain incomplete; the package is still partial and unreviewed. References are link-only: for earlier primary references, six abstracts were read and one scanned paper was not read in full, as documented in their source entries; the MIT calendar, syllabus and selected phage/genome/population notes were checked for this expansion. No source text, problem or dataset was imported. All family, population, association and testcross data are synthetic; no lesson gives genetic-counselling or medical advice, and nothing here says anything about any person's risk. Qualified human review is required before this course could meet the complete-course standard.
