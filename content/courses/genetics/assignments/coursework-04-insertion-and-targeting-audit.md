## Part A: an insertion series and its polarity

A synthetic three-gene operon is mutagenized by a terminator-bearing insertion element. Four stipulated mutants carry insertions: M1 in gene 1, M2 in gene 2, M3 in gene 3 and M4 again in gene 2. Expression assays show gene-3 transcripts collapsing in M1, M2 and M4, while an M3-derived transcript 5-prime of its insertion site persists. Classify each: M1, M2 and M4 are polar insertions — upstream landings that destroy gene 3's transcription without touching gene 3 — three of the four, while M3 is the single direct gene-3 disruption. A plasmid carrying only wild-type gene 3 complements the gene-3-dependent phenotype in all four mutants, but rescue of a phenotype does not say which lesion caused it; state in the report which assay distinguishes mechanism from rescue, because complementation alone cannot.

## Part B: a targeting audit

Stipulate four times ten million electroporated stem cells, a homologous targeting frequency of 2.5×10⁻⁶ and a random integration frequency of 2.5×10⁻⁴. Positive selection keeps every integrant: about 100 targeted colonies and 10,000 random ones. Negative selection against the outside marker kills a stipulated 98 percent of random integrants, leaving about 200 random survivors. The double-resistant pool is therefore about 300 colonies, of which one third — 33.3 percent — is correctly targeted, a 33.7-fold enrichment over the pre-selection fraction of 0.99 percent. Verify each candidate by DNA analysis anyway; enrichment is not identification, and a pooled number is not a clone's genotype.

## Part C: the chimera route

The confirmed targeted clone contributes to a chimera with a stipulated 40 percent donor coat contribution. A testcross of the chimera expects about 40 percent donor-colored offspring if and only if the contribution reached the germline; the cross is the test, not the coat, because somatic mosaicism and heritable transmission are separate facts about the same animal. Crossing two confirmed heterozygous carriers yields 25.0 percent homozygous replacement, 50.0 percent heterozygous and 25.0 percent wild type, stipulated percentages that the report should re-derive rather than quote, because a copied ratio is not an audited one.

## Part D: the conditional plan

The construct instead floxes exon 4 with two loxP sites per allele, and Cre is driven in one stipulated tissue. Predict the allele structure per tissue before any assay: in Cre-active tissue the flanked exon is gone and the allele is null there; in every other tissue the floxed allele remains functionally wild type. A homozygous floxed cell therefore carries four loxP sites in total, two per allele, and Cre changes that count only where its promoter is active. The plan's claims live or die on exon choice, promoter choice and timing — write those boundaries down before drawing conclusions, and keep the prediction separate from the outcome.
