# General Chemistry I scope crosswalk

This crosswalk compares the local package, version 0.7.0, with two openly published first-year chemistry sequences: MIT OpenCourseWare 5.111SC Principles of Chemical Science, Fall 2014 ([lecture list](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/lecture-videos/), 35 lectures in five units), and UC Irvine Chem 1A, 1B and 1C General Chemistry, a three-quarter sequence taught by three instructors ([Chem 1A](https://ocw.uci.edu/courses/chem_1a_general_chemistry.html), 23 lectures; [Chem 1B](https://ocw.uci.edu/courses/chem_1b_general_chemistry.html), 17 lectures; [Chem 1C](https://ocw.uci.edu/courses/chem_1c_general_chemistry.html), 26 lectures). It was compiled with AI assistance on 2026-10-28 from each comparator's public lecture titles, from the lecture descriptions of MIT Lectures 3, 4 and 11, from the Chem 1A topic index published with that course, and from the local readings and practice. It is an agent-run comparison, not a review by the owner or by a chemistry instructor. It does not compare lecture content beyond those descriptions, problem sets, exams or workload, and topic alignment establishes no equivalence, credit or endorsement. The earlier [General Chemistry I/II scope crosswalk](../../../docs/GENERAL_CHEMISTRY_SEQUENCE_CROSSWALK.md) compares both local packages with MIT 5.111SC unit by unit and is not repeated here.

Two scope notes apply. The local General Chemistry I and II packages divide one MIT sequence, so topics that MIT and UC Irvine place in Chem 1B or 1C are often taught in General Chemistry II, and the table marks those **elsewhere**. UC Irvine's sequence also covers topics MIT's does not, such as liquids and solids. Status labels: **covered** means a local reading develops the topic with a worked example and practice; **shallow** means a local reading teaches part of the topic; **absent** means no local instruction; **elsewhere** means another local course teaches it; **local extension** means the package teaches a topic neither comparator names. Local numbers are lesson IDs (general-chemistry-1-N), and each links to its reading.

## What 0.7.0 changed

Both comparators teach the quantum foundations of the atom, and the earlier sequence crosswalk had recorded MIT's wave and particle treatment as missing. No local reading used the photoelectric effect, the de Broglie wavelength, the uncertainty principle or a particle in a box. A new week-2 reading, lesson 21, develops them: it applies the photon picture to the photoelectric effect, uses the de Broglie and uncertainty relations to decide when wave behaviour matters, and derives the energies, absorption wavelengths and probabilities of a particle in a one-dimensional box from the standing-wave condition. The Schrödinger equation is named and its box solutions used, but it is not derived or solved in general.

Both comparators also teach the exceptions to the octet rule, which the local bonding lesson omitted. A new week-4 reading, lesson 22, keeps the valence-electron counting recipe and shows where incomplete octets, odd-electron species and expanded valence shells arise. It extends the shape predictions to steric numbers 5 and 6 and chooses between competing structures with formal charge, without attributing the bonding to d-orbital hybrids. Together the readings add six objectives, 26 practice items and eight retrieval cards.

## Remaining gaps

- Liquids, solids, vapor pressure and phase equilibria, and fuller intermolecular-force treatment, named only by UC Irvine (1A Lecture 16; 1B Lectures 1–4, 10, 12–13).
- Effusion, the van der Waals equation and black-body radiation, named only by UC Irvine.
- Entropy, the second and third laws and microstates, which MIT (Lectures 16–17) and UC Irvine (1B Lectures 7–9) both teach and which neither local course develops; this belongs to a General Chemistry II pass.
- Nuclear chemistry (MIT only) and nomenclature (UC Irvine only).
- Graded homework and exams, measured workload, and review by a qualified person.
