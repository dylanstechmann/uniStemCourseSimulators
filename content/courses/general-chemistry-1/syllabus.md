# General Chemistry I

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.4.10.** This personal hobby and learning project has substantial AI assistance and is not an accredited university course.

Chemical accounting, electronic structure, bonding models, energy and equilibrium are developed through original explanation and synthetic examples, with explicit units, precision and assumptions. In this local sequence, week-13 buffers and the calibration lab establish introductory buffer and measurement ideas; General Chemistry II develops coupled solution speciation, kinetics, redox, coordination and complexation. The two packages divide one MIT 5.111SC comparator and do not claim equivalence to two separate MIT courses.

## Prerequisites

Algebra is required; high-school chemistry is recommended. Manifest knowledge associations remain provisional and establish no university equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Balance reactions and quantify limiting reagents.
- Relate electronic structure, bonding, and molecular geometry to chemical properties.
- Use energy conservation and equilibrium models.
- Check dimensions and significant figures in calculations.

## Proposed 10-week sequence

This is a planning sequence, not measured workload or a reviewed semester. Four compact foundations now sit in the same teaching week as their expanded stoichiometry, thermochemistry or equilibrium treatment; the first reading is narrowed to electronic structure and periodic trends, with Lewis/VSEPR practice located in the bonding lesson. The molecular-orbital sequence and a valence-bond/hybridization reading in the same week fill distinct undergraduate bonding gaps. Ten teaching weeks retain all 14 readings and the existing synthetic lab; this is not evidence of semester equivalence. All activities are public formative practice.

## Assessment policy

There are 15 scheduled activities (14 readings and one synthetic lab), 113 public practice items and 58 retrieval cards, plus the preserved case. Numeric, single-choice, CSV-upload, structured-choice and multiple-select checks provide selected feedback with unlimited retries. Some numeric checks require units and specified significant figures; others state a bare-number scale and tolerance. Points are study feedback, not an institutional grade or credit. Written derivations and discussion criteria are not automatically graded. Assessment outcome lists reflect the objectives their items assess; prototype associations remain provisional.
