# Assignment 2: bonding and measure evidence

## Evidence question

How does overlapping-basis normalization change a small electronic-energy calculation, and what can that calculation actually establish? Use a distinct stipulated two-function model with real overlap S=0.1, equal diagonal element alpha=−2E* and off-diagonal element beta=−0.5E*. The functions are individually normalized and independent. These parameters define a teaching Hamiltonian and overlap matrix; they are not fitted properties of a chemical species.

Prepare the projected equations, normalized amplitudes, an occupancy ledger and a comparison of model alternatives. Retain all work locally. The browser checks selected values and interpretations, not the derivation or quality of your evidence discussion. No molecule is synthesized, no laboratory measurement is implied and no software package is required.

## Task A: derive rather than assume normalization

Write psi=c_A phi_A+c_B phi_B and integrate its squared amplitude, retaining the cross term. Derive the coefficients for equal-sign and opposite-sign combinations and show both norms equal one. Evaluate their mutual scalar product and explain why orthogonality of these combinations does not mean the original basis functions had zero overlap.

For this model the equal-sign coefficient magnitude is 1/sqrt2.2 and the opposite-sign magnitude is 1/sqrt1.8. A coefficient magnitude above or below 1/sqrt2 does not violate normalization, since probabilities cannot be obtained by independently squaring overlapping basis coefficients and adding them. Show explicitly how the cross term completes each normalization. Draw a possible amplitude cancellation in the opposite-sign combination without claiming that a cartoon uniquely determines the stipulated matrix elements.

## Task B: solve the projected energy equations

Insert c_B=c_A and c_B=−c_A into both projected equations. Obtain E_plus=−25E*/11 and E_minus=−5E*/3. Check each equation with the corresponding coefficient ratio. Also solve the determinant condition (alpha−E)²−(beta−ES)²=0 and verify that its two factors reproduce the same states. This second route is an independent algebraic check, not a second physical model.

Now set S to zero while retaining alpha and beta. Calculate the changed energies and compare them with the original values. Explain why omitting S does not merely discard a small displayed decimal: it changes normalization and the energy problem. Conversely, investigate a basis phase change phi_B→−phi_B. Change S and beta together and show that the set of energies is unchanged, with the labels of the combinations exchanged as appropriate. An isolated sign flip in only one matrix entry is not the same operation.

## Task C: occupy the states and preserve particle number

Place two opposite-spin electrons in the lower state and one electron in the upper state. The independent-electron energy sum is −205E*/33. The occupancy bond-order indicator is one half. Calculate the difference from a stipulated three-electron reference with energy 3alpha=−6E*: the model sum is lower by 7E*/33. Label this as an electronic comparison within the stipulated model, rather than a molecular dissociation energy.

List omitted terms that would be needed for the latter claim, including nuclear repulsion and electron interactions. Explain why comparing the same three-electron sum with a two-electron reference would fail particle accounting before any issue of model realism arose. Distinguish a formally allowed two-spin occupancy from a claim that a chosen state is the ground state of a real molecular ion.

## Task D: connect structure and observable claims

Prepare a table that assigns each question to an appropriate description: electron-domain geometry, localized hybrid overlap, delocalized state occupancy or a full energy curve. Include molecular shape, the number of sigma and pi connections in a supplied drawing you construct, a node in a superposition, and a measured bond dissociation enthalpy. State the inputs missing from the small two-function model for each stronger claim.

As a counterexample, compare two hypothetical models that have the same occupancy-based bond-order indicator but different alpha, beta or overlap. Show numerically that their electronic energies can differ. The indicator is useful within its declared purpose and is not a universal energy calculator. Keep the example distinct from the given rehearsal parameters and label any values you invent.
