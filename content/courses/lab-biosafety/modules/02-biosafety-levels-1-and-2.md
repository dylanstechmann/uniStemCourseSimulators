# Biosafety Levels 1 and 2

A Biosafety Level (BSL) is a combination of practices, safety equipment and facility features matched to the risks of the work. Most teaching, research and stem-cell laboratories work at BSL-1 or BSL-2, so these two levels are worth knowing item by item. This lesson follows Sections III and IV of BMBL, 6th edition (CDC and NIH, 2020), a US federal work adapted here with attribution, and explains what each element is for.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Distinguish primary barriers (safety equipment and personal protective equipment) from secondary barriers (facility design), and explain how practice, equipment and facility combine.
2. State the three main ways BSL-2 differs from BSL-1, and list the information posted at a BSL-2 entrance.
3. Apply the BSL-2 standard microbiological practices for gloves, hand hygiene, food, pipetting, sharps, aerosols, spills and waste.
4. Identify the BSL-2 special practices, safety equipment and facility features, and decide when a procedure must move into a biosafety cabinet.

## Three elements of containment

BMBL describes containment as three overlapping elements.

- **Laboratory practice and technique**: the most important element. Trained people following procedures prevent most exposures.
- **Safety equipment (primary barriers)**: physical containment placed directly at the level of the hazard, such as biological safety cabinets, enclosed containers and sealed rotors. Personal protective equipment (PPE) is also a primary barrier and becomes the main one where a cabinet cannot be used.
- **Facility design and construction (secondary barriers)**: features that protect people outside the laboratory and the environment, such as doors, a handwashing sink, cleanable surfaces and, at higher levels, directional airflow.

## BSL-1

BSL-1 is the basic level, for well-characterized agents not known to consistently cause disease in immunocompetent adult humans, which present minimal potential hazard to personnel and the environment. Work can be done on open benches with standard microbiological practices. Its facility needs are simple: doors for access control, a handwashing sink, an eyewash station, cleanable surfaces with no carpet, benchtops impervious to water and resistant to chemicals, and screens on windows that open.

## What BSL-2 adds

BMBL states that BSL-2 builds on BSL-1 and suits agents associated with human disease that pose moderate hazards. It differs from BSL-1 mainly in three ways:

1. personnel receive specific training in handling pathogenic agents and are supervised by scientists competent with those agents;
2. access to the laboratory is restricted while work is being done;
3. all procedures that can create infectious aerosols or splashes are done in biological safety cabinets or other physical containment equipment.

A sign with the universal biohazard symbol is posted at the entrance when infectious materials are present. It shows the laboratory's Biosafety Level, the name and telephone number of the supervisor or another responsible person, the PPE required, general occupational health requirements such as immunizations or respiratory protection, and the procedures for entering and leaving.

## Standard microbiological practices at BSL-2

The standard practices are the everyday habits. The main ones in BMBL's list are:

- **Training and supervision.** The supervisor ensures training on the hazards and precautions, keeps records, and provides annual updates and extra training when equipment, procedures or policies change.
- **Safety manual.** A facility-specific manual describes the containment and decontamination procedures in use, and the emergency procedures for exposures, medical emergencies and facility malfunctions.
- **Gloves.** Chosen by risk assessment, not worn outside the laboratory, changed when contaminated or damaged, and never washed or reused.
- **PPE removal and hand hygiene.** PPE is removed in a way that limits self-contamination, and hands are washed after handling hazardous materials and before leaving.
- **No eating, drinking, smoking, handling contact lenses or applying cosmetics**, and no food stored in the laboratory.
- **No mouth pipetting**: mechanical pipetting devices only.
- **Sharps.** Plasticware replaces glass where possible; needles are used only where there is no alternative, safety-engineered where possible, and never bent, sheared, recapped or removed by hand. Used needles go straight into a puncture-resistant container placed close to the point of use. Broken glass is picked up with a brush and dustpan, tongs or forceps, never by hand.
- **Minimize splashes and aerosols** in every procedure.
- **Decontaminate work surfaces** after work and after any spill or splash, and follow the posted spill procedure.
- **Decontaminate cultures and stocks** before disposal. Material leaving the laboratory for decontamination travels in a durable, leak-proof, closed container with a disinfected outer surface and a biohazard label.
- **Pest control**, and no animals or plants unrelated to the work.

## Special practices, equipment and facilities at BSL-2

**Special practices.** Access is controlled during work, and the supervisor confirms that staff are proficient. Medical surveillance and available immunizations are offered as appropriate. Properly maintained cabinets or other containment devices are used whenever procedures can create infectious aerosols or splashes, or when high concentrations or large volumes are handled. BMBL lists pipetting, centrifuging, grinding, blending, shaking, mixing, sonicating and opening containers of infectious material. High-concentration material may be centrifuged on the open bench only in sealed rotors or safety cups that are loaded and unloaded in the cabinet. Equipment is decontaminated routinely, after spills and before repair or removal. A validated method for decontaminating all laboratory waste is available. Incidents that may cause exposure are evaluated immediately, reported to the supervisor and recorded.

**Safety equipment.** Laboratory coats or gowns are worn for work and removed before going to offices, libraries or cafeterias, and they are not taken home. Eye and face protection is worn wherever splashes or sprays are possible. The risk assessment decides whether respiratory protection is needed, and anyone who needs it is enrolled in a respiratory protection program.

**Facilities.** BSL-2 adds self-closing, lockable doors, a handwashing sink near the exit, and vacuum lines protected by liquid disinfectant traps and in-line HEPA filters. Windows that open are not recommended.

## Common mistakes

- Wearing gloves to answer the door or the phone, or wearing a lab coat to the cafeteria.
- Recapping a needle "just this once" by hand.
- Vortexing or opening tubes of a BSL-2 culture on the open bench.
- Centrifuging in sealed cups but opening them on the bench.
- Treating the biohazard sign as decoration rather than information for anyone entering.

## Worked example

**Problem.** A new researcher in a BSL-2 laboratory plans to (a) streak plates of a BSL-2 bacterium, (b) vortex a dense suspension of it, (c) centrifuge 50 mL of the suspension, and (d) record results at a desk inside the laboratory. Which steps need a cabinet or other containment, and what else applies?

**Step (a).** Streaking makes little aerosol, but under the risk assessment it is usually done in the cabinet as well. Coat, gloves and eye protection are worn either way.

**Step (b).** Vortexing a dense suspension creates aerosols, so it is done in the cabinet with the tube capped.

**Step (c).** Large volumes at high concentration require sealed rotors or safety cups, loaded and opened in the cabinet.

**Step (d).** No food or drink at the desk, gloves removed before writing on shared surfaces, and hands washed before leaving the laboratory.

## Limits of this lesson

This lesson summarizes selected BSL-1 and BSL-2 criteria; the full lists in BMBL Section IV, your institution's biosafety manual and your biosafety officer govern real work. BSL-3, BSL-4 and the animal biosafety levels are not covered.
