# Project guide: a biosafety plan for an iPSC derivation project (optional, ungraded)

This optional project joins the six lessons into the document a laboratory writes before new work starts: a biosafety plan. The scenario is invented and all numbers are synthetic. The project is self-checked in the Case studio in five milestones and earns no points. It is practice in reasoning, not an approved plan: a real one is written with your biosafety officer and reviewed by your Institutional Biosafety Committee.

## The scenario

A laboratory will derive induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from peripheral blood of adult donors whose infection status is unknown. Blood (10 mL per donor) is processed to isolate mononuclear cells by density-gradient centrifugation. The cells are reprogrammed with a non-integrating viral vector supplied as a concentrated stock, expanded in culture, frozen, and some lines are sent to a collaborator in another state. The laboratory has a Class II Type A2 cabinet, a centrifuge with sealed safety cups, an autoclave and household bleach labelled 6.0%.

## Milestone 1: risk assessment

List the agent hazards (the donor material, the vector) and the procedure hazards (centrifugation, pipetting, opening tubes, sharps if any). Score each task's probability and consequence on 1–5 scales and rank them; then apply controls in hierarchy order and rescore. Say who must review the assessment before work starts and what would make you revisit it.

## Milestone 2: containment level and practices

State the minimum Biosafety Level and justify it from BMBL Appendix H. List the BSL-2 practices that matter most for this work, which procedures must be in the cabinet, and how centrifugation will be done. Name the OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard obligations that apply to staff on this project, with their timing.

## Milestone 3: the cabinet

The cabinet's front opening is 4.0 ft wide with an 8.0 in sash. Compute the inflow volume needed for the Type A2 minimum of 100 lfm, and the face velocity if a user raises the sash to 10.0 in without changing the airflow. The certification label says 13 months. What do you do before the project starts?

## Milestone 4: decontamination and waste

Plan surface disinfection and spill response using hypochlorite. Compute the bleach volume for 1.00 L of 5,000 ppm from the 6.0% stock. Waste culture is autoclaved; in a validation run the centre of a typical load reached 121 °C 15 min after the chamber. Using biological indicators of 10⁶ spores with D₁₂₁ = 2.0 min, compute the minimum chamber time for a twelve-log reduction at the centre, and say how routine cycles will be verified.

## Milestone 5: exposures and shipping

Write the first-minutes procedure for a needlestick and for an eye splash, and who is called. Then describe how the frozen iPSC lines will be shipped to the collaborator: who classifies and packs them, and what triple packaging consists of.

## Worked example with different numbers

A different laboratory has a 5.0 ft cabinet opening with a 10.0 in sash, a 7.5% bleach stock and a waste load that lags 20 min with D₁₂₁ = 1.5 min.

- **Inflow.** A = 5.0 × 10.0/12 = 4.17 ft², so Q = 100 × 4.17 = 417 cubic feet per minute.
- **Bleach.** 7.5% ≈ 75,000 ppm; for 500 mL of 5,000 ppm, V₁ = 500 × 5,000/75,000 = 33.3 mL.
- **Autoclave.** 20 + 12 × 1.5 = 38 min at 121 °C as the minimum chamber time for the centre of that load.
- **Ranking.** Opening centrifuge tubes of donor cells on the bench scores 4 × 3 = 12; opening sealed cups in the cabinet scores 2 × 3 = 6.

## What to hand in to yourself

A two-page plan: the risk table before and after controls, the containment level and key practices, the cabinet checks, the decontamination and waste plan with its calculations, and the exposure and shipping procedures. Compare it with the milestone checklists in the Case studio.

## Limits of this project

The scenario, scores and numbers are synthetic. Real projects with human donor material and viral vectors need institutional review, donor consent and material agreements, occupational health enrolment and trained shippers, none of which this exercise provides.
