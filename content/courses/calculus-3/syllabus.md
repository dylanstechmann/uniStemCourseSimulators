# Calculus III: Multivariable & Vector Calculus

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.7.0.** This personal hobby and learning project is developed with substantial AI assistance. It is not an accredited university course.

Multivariable functions, geometry and vector fields describe spatial change and accumulation. Synthetic constructions develop coordinate measures, optimization, circulation, transfer and conservation. See the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) for the comparison with MIT OCW 18.02SC and Harvard Math 21a.

## Prerequisites

Calculus II is required; linear algebra is recommended. Manifest prerequisites are provisional authoring associations and establish no university equivalency. Vector and coordinate calculations use the stated Cartesian scales and angle conventions.

## Authored outcomes

- Represent vectors in frames, compute directional derivatives, and interpret gradients.
- Optimize multivariable functions with and without constraints.
- Set up multiple integrals in appropriate coordinates.
- Relate line and surface integrals to vector-field theorems.
- Verify branch, coordinate-coverage and integrability conditions before applying local derivatives, change of variables or integral theorems.

## Connected coursework (open, ungraded)

Three original foundations develop implicit branches and local charts (week 3), nonlinear Jacobians and global coordinate coverage (week 8), and signed singular integrals, order and cutoff hypotheses (week 9). Four reports connect implicit finite changes and global extrema (week 5), nonlinear coordinate measures and repeated coverage (week 10), complete curved-boundary flux, circulation and scalar area (week 12), and a domain/limit dossier (week 14). Twenty checkpoints/cards assess selected values and interpretations. Continuous fields request three significant figures; exact branch, preimage and maximizer counts have no precision penalty. Derivations, proofs, plots, code and reports are retained locally and are not automatically evaluated. Counts and proposed weeks do not establish measured semester workload.
