# Surrogate-evidence dossier project guide (optional, ungraded)

**Status:** open, optional practice. You can save milestone responses privately in the Case studio; this route is ungraded.
**Introduced in:** course package 0.6.0.
**Suggested time (unvalidated author estimate):** 2–4 hours across weeks 14–14.

## Start here

An age-associated predictor, a biological response and a meaningful outcome are distinct evidence claims. This dossier makes the distinctions explicit in one comparison table and asks what survives a change in design or model context. The goal is a careful evidence map, not a ranking of interventions or a numerical biological-age claim. A supplied interval is interpreted with its endpoint and null before any biological conclusion is considered.

Work through the supplied project after the related course readings. First close the model or evidence ledger on paper, then use the separate rehearsal to test your method. The worked example uses different inputs and is a demonstration of the reasoning, not a response to the project. Keep definitions, units, denominators, signs and assumptions visible in your own response. If a check fails, return to the model rather than adjusting a number to match an option.

## Project brief and supplied information

The [project evidence table](surrogate-endpoint-evidence.csv) supplies three entirely fictional comparisons. Study A is an observational younger-versus-older age-group comparison in a model organism. B and C are randomized intervention-versus-control comparisons in different fictional model contexts. Marker units, functional assay points and survival hazard ratios describe different endpoints. Intervals are supplied synthetic 95% confidence intervals for interpretation, not estimates to reconstruct from individual observations.

| Study | Marker difference (95% CI) | Function difference (95% CI) | Survival HR (95% CI) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | +2.1 (1.4, 2.8) | −1.4 (−2.0, −0.8) | Not measured |
| B | −1.8 (−2.4, −1.2) | +0.2 (−0.5, +0.9) | 0.98 (0.78, 1.23) |
| C | +0.1 (−0.5, +0.7) | +1.1 (+0.4, +1.8) | 0.76 (0.60, 0.96) |

Use null zero for differences and one for hazard ratios. Hazard ratios are positive ratios; a value below one is not a negative hazard and does not directly specify a lifespan change. Identify the narrowest conclusion each design and interval supports, then evaluate the proposed surrogate claim across the contexts. A null-crossing interval does not prove zero effect, and two distinct models are not a head-to-head intervention comparison.

## Milestones and self-review

### Milestone 1: Endpoints, nulls and interval widths

For project studies A–C, label each endpoint and comparison, identify its null value and mark which supplied intervals exclude it. Keep marker units, functional assay points and survival hazard ratios separate.

- Uses the correct null for each effect scale.
- Distinguishes an unmeasured endpoint from an estimated interval crossing its null.
- Does not rank unlike endpoint scales by raw numerical size.

### Milestone 2: Association and intervention evidence

Classify project Study A’s design and write the narrowest association statement. Give one confounding or selection explanation compatible with its age association, then state why that comparison does not validate an intervention surrogate.

- Names the age-group comparison and observational design.
- Separates statistical association from an intervention or mediation claim.
- Identifies the new context and outcome evidence a surrogate claim would require.

### Milestone 3: A marker response without resolved benefit

Write the narrowest supported statement for project Study B, separating marker response from function and survival uncertainty. Explain why the marker result alone cannot stand in for a meaningful outcome and why the other intervals do not prove exact absence.

- Names the randomized intervention-versus-control comparison.
- Reports all endpoints rather than selecting only the marker result.
- Keeps unresolved intervals distinct from demonstrated equivalence.

### Milestone 4: Outcome signals without a resolved marker change

For project Study C, distinguish the marker, function and survival statements supported by their supplied intervals. Explain the challenge it poses to an unqualified universal marker surrogate claim, while keeping hazard, lifespan and human translation separate.

- States the functional and hazard-ratio evidence without inventing a duration effect.
- Describes a null-crossing marker interval as uncertain rather than exactly zero.
- Keeps the conclusion inside the fictional model comparison.

### Milestone 5: Surrogate validation and a bounded dossier

Assemble the project A–C evidence map. Decide what the dossier can and cannot establish about surrogate validity, name at least two missing forms of evidence, and add a two-sentence scope note preventing any fictional model-organism result from becoming a human-health or intervention recommendation.

- Integrates association, marker response and meaningful outcomes without collapsing their roles.
- Defines the intended context and names missing measurement/outcome validation evidence.
- Avoids head-to-head ranking, universal surrogacy and human-health conclusions unsupported by the dossier.

## Worked example: a separate case

Consider a separate fictional randomized intervention-versus-control comparison U. Its marker difference is −0.9 arbitrary units with 95% interval −1.4 to −0.4. Function difference is +0.7 assay points with interval +0.1 to +1.3. Survival HR is 0.88 with interval 0.70 to 1.11. The marker and function intervals exclude their null of zero, while the survival interval includes its null of one. State all three results rather than choosing the marker because it looks most favourable.

The marker interval width is 1.0 marker unit and the function interval width is 1.2 assay points. Those raw widths cannot rank precision without a justified shared scale. The hazard-ratio point estimate corresponds arithmetically to 1−0.88 = 0.12 lower relative hazard, but the interval still includes the null. That point estimate is not proof of a survival effect, and it is not a 12% increase in lifespan. A median survival or time-ratio claim would need different information about the survival distributions and their analysis.

Randomized allocation can support a causal comparison of the assigned intervention under its design and analysis assumptions; it does not automatically identify the marker as the mediator or a validated replacement endpoint. Even if marker and function both change in U, a common response to an intervention does not establish how a marker change predicts meaningful outcomes across other interventions. The measurement procedure, intended context, relevant outcomes and substantial outcome-linked validation evidence must be specified separately. Mechanistic plausibility or age prediction alone is not a replacement for that evidence.

Finally, write an evidence-map row: design randomized; context fictional U; endpoint/scale marker difference, function difference and survival ratio; supplied estimates and intervals as above; supported comparison-specific marker/function signals; unresolved survival effect, surrogate validity and transport to other models or humans. Every estimate here is constructed teaching data. The row evaluates a reasoning structure and does not recommend an intervention or describe an actual organismal result.

## Rehearsal: practice before the project

Use the [rehearsal dossier](surrogate-evidence-rehearsal.csv), which changes every comparison from the project. Y is a randomized comparison: marker −1.2 (−1.8,−0.6), function +0.1 (−0.4,+0.6), survival HR 1.02 (0.82,1.28). Z is randomized in a different fictional context: marker +0.2 (−0.3,+0.7), function +0.9 (+0.3,+1.5), HR 0.80 (0.65,0.98). All intervals are supplied 95% intervals, all values are invented and no pair is a controlled head-to-head comparison. Interpret the designs and nulls before doing the interval-width and ratio arithmetic.

## Finish and check your report

Include the supplied inputs, your equations or evidence map, intermediate quantities with units, a final table with clearly labelled rows and a short interpretation. For every numerical result, include a second check: substitute into the original relation, verify a conservation balance, compare a limiting case or independently recompute it. For evidence interpretations, name the comparison, design, endpoint, uncertainty and the conclusion that remains unsupported. A diagram or plot needs a plain-text description that preserves its scientific meaning; colour alone must not carry a distinction.

Use the Case studio checklist only after finding the corresponding evidence in your response. Marking a box records your own review; it does not score prose, certify mastery or establish an institutional grade. Disclose spreadsheets, code, references and AI assistance so another reader can distinguish your reasoning from external calculations. Keep a copy of your report and review any discrepancy between your calculation and the public rehearsal feedback before moving on.
