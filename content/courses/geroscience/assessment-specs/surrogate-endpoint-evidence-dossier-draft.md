# Surrogate-endpoint evidence dossier (inactive draft)

**Status: authoring draft only.** This fictional, ungraded paper exercise is not an assessment used by the course platform. It contains no real intervention, organismal result, treatment guidance or human-aging claim. All estimates and confidence intervals in the companion CSV are invented teaching values.

## Purpose

Use a small evidence dossier to distinguish an age-associated predictor, a marker response, tissue function and survival. The task extends the biomarker and evidence-tier readings; it does not repeat the lifespan lab's censoring calculations or the study-design case's proposal-writing work.

## Dossier

The CSV contains three fictional comparisons. Study A is a cross-sectional age-group comparison in a model organism. Studies B and C are randomized intervention-versus-control comparisons in two different fictional model contexts. Marker values are arbitrary assay units, functional differences are standardized assay points, and survival effects are hazard ratios. Reported intervals are synthetic 95% confidence intervals; they are supplied for interpretation, not recalculation. The functional scale and biological context need not be comparable between studies.

| Study | Context and design | Marker estimate (95% CI) | Function estimate (95% CI) | Survival HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | Model organism, younger vs older age groups; observational | +2.1 (1.4, 2.8) | −1.4 (−2.0, −0.8) | Not measured |
| B | Fictional strain A, intervention X vs control; randomized | −1.8 (−2.4, −1.2) | +0.2 (−0.5, +0.9) | 0.98 (0.78, 1.23) |
| C | Fictional strain B, intervention Y vs control; randomized | +0.1 (−0.5, +0.7) | +1.1 (+0.4, +1.8) | 0.76 (0.60, 0.96) |

The first two estimates in each row are mean differences for that row's stated comparison. A hazard ratio below one would mean a lower event hazard in the intervention group, if that synthetic model and analysis were valid. The confidence intervals crossing the null indicate uncertainty; they do not prove exact absence of an effect.

## Learner task

1. For each row, mark which marker, function and survival comparisons are distinguishable from their null value using the supplied interval. State the null value used for each measure.
2. Classify Study A as evidence of association or intervention causality. Name one reason its age association cannot establish that changing the marker changes function.
3. Write the narrowest result statement supported by Study B. Explain why its marker result alone does not establish benefit in function or survival.
4. Write the narrowest result statement supported by Study C. Explain what its marker result says about using the marker as a universal surrogate across these fictional contexts.
5. Decide whether this three-row dossier validates the marker as a surrogate endpoint. Identify at least two additional forms of evidence needed before such a claim could be supported, and state the highest evidence tier represented here.
6. Add a two-sentence scope note that prevents the model-organism examples from being presented as a human-health conclusion.

## Author reference

- Study A: marker and function intervals exclude zero, but the design is cross-sectional. It supports an age-group association in this synthetic context, not an intervention effect or mediation claim.
- Study B: the marker interval excludes zero; function and hazard-ratio intervals cross their nulls (zero difference and HR 1, respectively). This supports a marker shift in the fictional randomized comparison, with function and survival effects unresolved.
- Study C: marker interval crosses zero; function interval excludes zero; hazard-ratio interval excludes 1. The synthetic intervention comparison therefore has functional and survival signals without a detectable marker change.
- The combined examples argue against treating this marker as a validated, context-independent surrogate. They do not constitute formal validation or disproof: there are only two invented intervention contexts, different models, endpoints and scales. Replicated randomized studies across multiple interventions that measure both marker and meaningful outcomes, together with evidence that intervention effects track through the marker, would be needed for a stronger surrogate claim.
- All examples stop at fictional model-organism evidence. They support no claim about a real intervention, clinical outcome or human aging.
