# Organic Chemistry I scope crosswalk

This crosswalk compares the local package, version 0.7.0, with two openly published undergraduate courses: MIT OpenCourseWare 5.12 Organic Chemistry I, Spring 2005 ([calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-12-organic-chemistry-i-spring-2005/pages/calendar/)), and UCI OpenCourseWare Chem 51A Organic Chemistry, taught by James S. Nowick in Fall 2009 ([course page](https://open.uci.edu/courses/chem_51a_organic_chemistry_nowick.html)). It was compiled with AI assistance on 2026-10-26 and 2026-10-27 from each comparator's public session and lecture titles and from the local readings and practice. It is an agent-run comparison, not a review by the owner or by a chemistry instructor. It does not compare lecture content, problem sets, exams or workload, and topic alignment establishes no equivalence, credit or endorsement.

Chem 51A is the first course of UCI's three-quarter organic sequence, so it bears only on structure, acid–base chemistry, conformations, stereochemistry, reaction energetics, substitution and elimination; its later quarters are not compared. Status labels: **covered** means a local reading develops the topic with a worked example and practice; **shallow** means the topic appears only in passing or as part of another topic; **absent** means no local instruction; **local extension** means the package teaches a topic neither comparator names in its titles. Local numbers are lesson IDs (organic-chemistry-N), and each links to its reading.

## What 0.7.0 changed

Reaction energetics was the one topic that both comparators teach over several lectures and the package lacked. The new week-2 reading follows the conformations lesson and precedes substitution, the position both comparators give it. It treats free energy and equilibrium for conformer, acid–base and reaction equilibria, enthalpy and entropy, bond-energy estimates, barriers and rates, energy diagrams and rate-determining steps, the Hammond postulate, kinetic versus thermodynamic control, and catalysts. It adds three objectives, 13 practice items and four retrieval cards, and links General Chemistry II, a prerequisite course, for the general thermodynamic and kinetic relations.

## Remaining gaps

- Alkane nomenclature, named only by UCI Chem 51A's titles.
- Alcohol and phenol reactions as a family, named only by MIT 5.12's titles.
- Intermolecular forces and physical properties of functional groups.
- Graded homework and exams, measured workload, and review by a qualified person.
