# Aldol-identity dossier project guide (optional, ungraded)

**Status:** open, optional practice. You can save milestone responses privately in the Case studio; this route is ungraded.
**Introduced in:** course package 0.5.0.
**Suggested time (unvalidated author estimate):** 2–4 hours across weeks 11–12.

## Start here

A proposed structure, a formula and an analytical observation answer different questions. This dossier joins them without collapsing compatibility into proof. It begins with a deliberately stipulated atom map, checks the formula at each step, accounts for unsaturation and proton totals, and then names the stereochemical and quantitative information that is still missing. A new carbon-carbon bond is not, by itself, evidence of selectivity or an experimental product.

Work through the supplied project after the related course readings. First close the model or evidence ledger on paper, then use the separate rehearsal to test your method. The worked example uses different inputs and is a demonstration of the reasoning, not a response to the project. Keep definitions, units, denominators, signs and assumptions visible in your own response. If a check fails, return to the model rather than adjusting a number to match an option.

## Project brief and supplied information

Use the stipulated paper addition of propanal CH3–CH2–CHO as donor and benzaldehyde Ph–CHO as acceptor. Reactant formulas are C3H6O and C7H6O. Assume the one-donor/one-acceptor crossed connectivity only for the atom-mapping exercise; no conditions or competing-product amounts are specified. Map the donor alpha carbon to the acceptor carbonyl carbon, retain the donor aldehyde and describe the acceptor oxygen after proton transfer.

Treat addition and a subsequent loss of one H2O equivalent as distinct steps. For the resulting neutral C/H/O formulas use DBE = (2C+2−H)/2 and reconcile the count with the phenyl, carbonyl and any alkene features. The [project evidence cards](crossed-aldol-evidence-cards.csv) supply synthetic qualitative carbonyl/OH compatibility and proton integrals. They provide no chemical shifts, couplings, response factors, calibrated component amounts or absolute-configuration information. Sum the counts and test compatibility with the stated candidates; do not turn the observation cards into proof of unique identity, purity or yield.

## Milestones and self-review

### Milestone 1: Map the donor and acceptor

For project propanal donor and benzaldehyde acceptor, label donor carbonyl, donor alpha carbon and acceptor carbonyl. Draw or describe the stipulated beta-hydroxy connectivity, including the fate of each oxygen. This is a paper mapping, not a claim of actual reaction selectivity.

- Identifies the alpha carbon by adjacency to the donor carbonyl.
- Connects it to the acceptor carbonyl carbon while preserving the donor aldehyde.
- Describes the structure in words as well as any drawing.

### Milestone 2: Separate addition from dehydration

Add the project reactant formulas C3H6O and C7H6O, then separately subtract one H2O for the dehydrated candidate. Check carbon, hydrogen and oxygen individually and identify which step changes each count.

- Conserves both reactants’ atoms in the addition formula.
- Subtracts exactly H2O only for the separate dehydration.
- Checks atom counts against the drawn or verbally described connectivity.

### Milestone 3: Unsaturation ledger

Calculate both project formula unsaturation counts, then account for each ring and multiple bond in the proposed structures. Keep the formula arithmetic separate from any claim that the formula uniquely identifies a structure.

- Applies the formula to the correct hydrogen count for each candidate.
- Accounts for aromatic, carbonyl and newly introduced alkene units.
- Does not equate a matching DBE with unique molecular identity.

### Milestone 4: Evidence cards and proton totals

Use project evidence cards A and B to sum proton counts and compare the OH/carbonyl constraints with the two candidate formulas. State the observation model and at least two alternative claims that the supplied evidence cannot establish.

- Adds every stated integral once and compares the total with its formula.
- Treats a missing qualitative feature as conditional on the supplied observation model.
- Separates structural compatibility from calibrated amount, purity and yield.

### Milestone 5: Stereochemical questions and scope

Identify the project addition product’s stereogenic centers and the additional E/Z question after dehydration. Explain exactly which assignments remain undetermined. Close the report by separating the stipulated atom map from real conversion, selectivity and synthesis claims.

- Finds stereogenic elements from distinct substituents rather than from formula alone.
- Keeps a count of centers distinct from assigned R/S or E/Z labels.
- States the absence of conditions, calibrated amounts and real-product evidence.

## Worked example: a separate case

Use a different donor, acetaldehyde CH3–CHO (C2H4O), with benzaldehyde (C7H6O), under a stipulated one-donor/one-acceptor paper connectivity. Label the acetaldehyde alpha carbon D2, its carbonyl D1 and the acceptor carbonyl A1. The new bond is D2–A1. A text description of the addition product is O=CH–CH2–CH(OH)–Ph: D1 remains an aldehyde carbonyl, D2 becomes the intervening methylene and A1 becomes the hydroxyl-bearing methine. No experimental conditions or product proportions follow from that diagram.

Add atoms before considering dehydration: C2H4O + C7H6O = C9H10O2. The degree of unsaturation is (18+2−10)/2 = 5. A phenyl group provides four units and the aldehyde carbonyl provides one. A hypothetical card with aromatic integral 5, alpha-methylene integral 2, carbinol integral 1, aldehyde integral 1 and exchangeable-OH integral 1 sums to ten hydrogens. These are deliberately constructed constraints; exchangeable proton integrals in a real observation would need their own measurement assumptions.

Subtract one water equivalent for the separate dehydrated candidate: C9H10O2 − H2O = C9H8O. Now DBE is (18+2−8)/2 = 6, with one additional alkene. The text connectivity is O=CH–CH=CH–Ph; five aromatic, two vinylic and one aldehyde hydrogen sum to eight. Reversing the formula subtraction by adding H2O must recover the addition formula, which provides an independent atom-balance check.

The addition example has one stereogenic center at the carbinol carbon. Its donor alpha carbon is CH2 with two identical H substituents, so it is not a second center. The dehydrated alkene raises an E/Z question, but these formulas and integral totals do not assign it. The change in donor makes this a useful contrast with the project rather than a completed project response. The evidence remains a compatibility test under a stipulated structure and observation model, not a reaction procedure or real sample identification.

## Rehearsal: practice before the project

Use butanal CH3–CH2–CH2–CHO (C4H8O) as donor and 4-methylbenzaldehyde, para-CH3–C6H4–CHO (C8H8O), as acceptor. The stipulated addition connectivity is O=CH–CH(CH2CH3)–CH(OH)–C6H4–CH3, with the ring methyl para to the side chain. The [rehearsal evidence cards](aldol-identity-rehearsal.csv) give X an OH-compatible feature and no vinylic proton; Y has no detected OH under the declared model and one vinylic proton. Both retain an aldehyde-carbonyl-compatible feature. Each row lists separate aromatic, aryl-methyl, ethyl-methyl, ethyl-methylene, alpha-methine, carbinol, vinylic, aldehyde and exchangeable-OH counts. Sum those columns rather than assuming the formula from a card label.

## Finish and check your report

Include the supplied inputs, your equations or evidence map, intermediate quantities with units, a final table with clearly labelled rows and a short interpretation. For every numerical result, include a second check: substitute into the original relation, verify a conservation balance, compare a limiting case or independently recompute it. For evidence interpretations, name the comparison, design, endpoint, uncertainty and the conclusion that remains unsupported. A diagram or plot needs a plain-text description that preserves its scientific meaning; colour alone must not carry a distinction.
