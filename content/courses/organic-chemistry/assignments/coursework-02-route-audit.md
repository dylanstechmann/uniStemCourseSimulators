# Assignment 2: audit a protected multistep route

**Open, ungraded paper synthesis and evidence practice, developed with substantial AI assistance.** Proposed placement: block 12. Work from structures, explicit rules and synthetic yields; this is not a preparation procedure. The browser checks selected calculations and choices but does not grade a mechanism drawing, implementation or written report.

## Part A: define a new target and its bond change

Use 3-acetylbenzaldehyde, a meta-substituted aromatic ring bearing one aldehyde and one methyl ketone. The target retains the aldehyde and converts the ketone into an alcohol carbon attached to the ring and two methyl groups. The intended new bond joins a methyl carbon to the former ketone carbonyl carbon.

Draw or describe both starting and target structures with labeled atoms. Count the ring hydrogens and substituent atoms, derive both neutral molecular formulas and calculate their degrees of unsaturation. Identify which carbonyl disappears in the target and which remains. A formula alone does not specify which group reacted; connect every count to the labeled structure.

Contrast the target with the reading's para-substituted ethyl-addition example. There the new alcohol carbon has aryl, methyl, ethyl and hydroxyl substituents. Here two of its substituents are methyl. Decide whether this assignment introduces a tetrahedral stereogenic center at that carbon and justify the answer by substituent identity rather than by counting drawn bonds.

## Part B: derive a compatibility-constrained route

Use three stipulated operations. P masks the aldehyde selectively while leaving the ketone available. N adds the methyl fragment to the exposed ketone and includes the necessary protonation step. D restores the aldehyde while retaining the desired alcohol. Assume the complete mask survives N only for this paper exercise; no actual selectivity data or operating conditions are supplied.

Enumerate all six orders of P,N,D. Apply the requirements that P precedes N, D follows N, and unmasking before N re-exposes an undesired carbonyl. Identify the valid order under those rules. For each rejected order, name a specific failed precondition instead of simply marking it “bad.”

For the valid route, describe the electrophile, nucleophile, tetrahedral intermediate and protonation in the bond-forming step. Explain which observations would be needed to establish that the mask was selective, that it survived and that its removal did not damage the new alcohol. A compatibility assumption is useful for a model but cannot substitute for those observations.

## Part C: close the temporary atom ledger

If the aldehyde mask is represented by a conceptual ethylene-glycol acetal, protection combines the starting molecule with C2H6O2 and formally removes H2O. Derive the protected formula. Add the methyl fragment and proton in N, then reverse the temporary mask contribution in D to recover the target formula.

Check atoms at every step. Explain why the final target does not retain the protecting group's carbons. Keep total-formula accounting distinct from isotope-resolved oxygen identity: reversible carbonyl/acetal exchange can require additional information for an atom-by-atom oxygen claim. No isotope experiment is specified here.

## Part D: compare route metrics without changing their definitions

Stipulate isolated fractional yields P=0.90, N=0.75 and D=0.80, starting from 3.00 mmol. Calculate overall isolated fraction and target amount. Explain why adding percentages is inappropriate and when multiplying another purity factor would double-count a correction already included in the stated yield.

For a separate route comparison, a four-step route gives 0.90 per step while a two-step route gives 0.80 and 0.75. Compare overall fractions and state what additional evidence would be needed to compare cost, selectivity, stereochemistry and practical suitability. Step count alone does not define route quality.

## Part E: an evidence-aware report

Propose a minimal set of complementary observations for checking loss of the targeted ketone, retention of the aldehyde, removal of the mask and the product's identity. Distinguish qualitative compatibility, calibrated component amounts, purity and structural uniqueness. Do not assign a single absolute configuration to a carbon with two identical methyl substituents.

Include text alternatives for drawings, a forward compatibility table, atom/DBE ledgers, an order audit and the yield calculation. Disclose references, calculations, software and AI assistance. A correct numeric checkpoint does not evaluate that complete reasoning chain.

## Self-review after attempting the assignment

The starting formula is C9H8O2 and the target formula C10H12O2. Their DBEs are six and five. The final alcohol carbon has two identical methyl groups, so this addition does not introduce a tetrahedral stereogenic center there. The stipulated yields give overall fraction 0.540 and target amount 1.62 mmol. The sequence P,N,D is the only valid one under the declared strict ordering rules.

These answers do not demonstrate that a real preparation would be selective or efficient. The paper route deliberately leaves those empirical questions open.
