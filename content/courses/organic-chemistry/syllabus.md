# Organic Chemistry I for Life Sciences

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.4.9.** Personal hobby and learning project with substantial AI assistance; no university equivalence or institutional credit.

Original instruction develops structure, electron flow, stereochemistry, carbonyl connectivity, reaction competition and analytical evidence with declared synthetic examples. One original text Fischer diagram includes a verbal description.

## Prerequisites

General Chemistry I and II. Structured manifest relationships remain provisional and establish no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Draw valid resonance contributors and predict nucleophile/electrophile roles.
- Use substrate, nucleophile, solvent, and leaving group to distinguish mechanisms.
- Track stereochemistry and regiochemistry.
- Relate functional-group reactivity to biomolecular synthesis and degradation.

## Proposed grouped content sequence

This 12-block sequence is planning scope, not measured workload or a reviewed semester. All four original prototype readings now carry expanded instruction, so no compact prototype readings remain; the sequence groups each with its related expanded reading and places a selective alkene/alkyne reaction block after elimination and before aromaticity. MIT 5.12 Spring 2005 spends calendar weeks 4–7 on alkenes and alkynes; this local two-reading block is narrower and does not claim course equivalence. The final block is a synthetic peak-calibration/excess lab with the preserved amide self-assessment case.

## Assessment policy

There are 17 course lessons including the synthetic-data lab, 125 public practice items and 63 retrieval cards, plus the preserved case. Each new lesson has four numeric and five conceptual choices; the lab includes CSV, numeric, structured-choice and multiple-select checks. Numeric items request a number in a stated scale except four preserved unit-required items. Significant figures, dimensions, drawn mechanisms and written arguments are not automatically scored. Unlimited retries provide study feedback, not a course grade. Outcome links still need qualified review.

## Current limitations

The four repeated primer/expanded calendar pairs are grouped, and the course now includes a selective two-reading alkene/alkyne addition block. MIT 5.12 assigns four weeks to alkene/alkyne reactions; the aromaticity lesson now pairs EAS with an SNAr addition–elimination strand, as the 5.12 week-11 calendar does, but benzyne chemistry, reagent breadth and multistep synthesis remain absent or thin locally. One data lab is not a laboratory sequence; graded homework and exams remain absent, and the project draft is inactive and ungraded. Workload, accessibility and chemical quality await review. New examples provide no chemical handling or synthesis procedure. Peak factors represent synthetic reporting gains, not an intrinsic enantiomer absorbance difference. The package remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only.
