# Physics: Electricity & Magnetism

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.4.8.** Personal hobby and learning project with substantial AI assistance; no university equivalence or institutional credit.

Original instruction develops field/potential signs, electrostatic boundaries, dielectric energy, circuit loading, RC states, magnetic vectors, magnetic-field sources and dipoles, induction, electrode-interface measurement, AC inference, displacement current, ideal plane-wave energy and coherent two-source interference using declared models. An original text connection map includes a verbal description. All 16 instructional readings have substantive instruction, alongside a synthetic data lab.

## Prerequisites

Calculus I; Physics: Mechanics recommended. Manifest relationships remain provisional and establish no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Use field and potential models for charge distributions.
- Analyze resistive-capacitive networks.
- Apply magnetic-force and induction laws.
- Relate circuit measurements to sensor loading and safety limits.
- Apply the Ampère–Maxwell law and Poynting energy flux to ideal time-varying fields and plane waves.
- Analyze coherent electromagnetic-wave interference using path difference, phase and the stated geometric assumptions.

## Proposed 14-week schedule

This is planning scope, not measured workload or a reviewed semester. Week 1 groups the related field/potential and superposition/Gauss readings; week 8 pairs magnetic vectors with Biot–Savart, Ampère's law and magnetic dipoles; week 11 adds displacement current, Ampère–Maxwell closure, ideal plane-wave energy flow and two-source interference after induction. Week 14 is a synthetic calibration/loading/time-constant lab and revisits the preserved high-impedance recording self-assessment case. All activities are public formative practice.

## Assessment policy

There are 16 instructional readings, one synthetic data lab, 118 public practice items and 60 retrieval cards, plus the preserved case. The Maxwell lesson has six numeric checks and two multiple-select items; the new interference lesson has six numeric checks, two multiple-select items and four cards. The lab includes CSV summaries, reporting/source/input calculations, structured choices and multiple-select interpretation. Fifteen of 82 numeric items require units: eight preserved items, three unit-checked current/energy-flux items and four unit-checked fringe/path calculations. Other new values use stated numerical scales. Significant figures, dimensions, diagrams, written arguments and uncertainty analyses are not automatically scored. Unlimited retries provide study feedback, not course grades. Outcome links still need qualified review.
