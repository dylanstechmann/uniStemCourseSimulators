## Part A: derive the forward comparison

Use a long cylindrical membrane model with zero spontaneous curvature, no pressure work or cortex, negligible end contributions and a fixed positive tension reservoir. Bending rigidity is kappa=50 pN nm and tension sigma=0.04 pN/nm. Starting from curvature and area, derive E/L=pi kappa/r+2pi sigma r rather than inserting a radius formula without its assumptions.

Differentiate with respect to positive r, find the stationary radius and check the second derivative. The radius is 25 nm and extension force is 4pi pN. Show that each energy-per-length contribution is 2pi pN at that optimum, so their sum equals the force derivative with respect to length.

Plot the normalized energy versus radius on a declared positive interval with labeled units and a textual description. Mark the optimum and compare independent values on either side. A coarse plot can suggest a minimum, but the derivative and admissibility checks establish the result for this stipulated function.

## Part C: perturb assumptions and preserve the target

At fixed kappa, vary sigma by a factor of four and verify the predicted half-radius/double-force scaling. Separately vary both coefficients while holding their product fixed, and compare the radii. These are different changes to the model, not interchangeable explanations of one observed force.

Discuss what would change if drawing a tube depleted a finite area reservoir and tension changed with length. An added area-reservoir energy or prescribed constraint would require a new derivative and possibly a new equilibrium radius. Do not keep the fixed-sigma force law unchanged after removing the condition that supported it.

Likewise, pressure work, spontaneous curvature, cortex attachment and viscous pulling can introduce different contributions. They are absent from this supplied equilibrium model. A discrepancy with the formula could arise from these assumptions rather than an incorrectly calculated kappa. The report should distinguish numerical residual, parameter uncertainty and model inadequacy.
