# Cellular Biomechanics & Mechanobiology

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.5.0.**

Connect forces, matrix mechanics, cell adhesion, mechanotransduction, and quantitative assays across molecular-to-tissue scales.

## Prerequisites

Cell biology; Physics: Mechanics; Differential Equations recommended. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Translate boundary conditions into stress and strain measures.
- Interpret viscoelastic and nonlinear material behavior.
- Connect integrin/cytoskeletal signaling to mechanical context.
- Design an experiment that separates stiffness from ligand density.
- Derive selected membrane bending/tension equilibria and identify their coefficients from independent force and geometry observations.
- Integrate a force-dependent rupture rate over a prescribed history and interpret survival, quantiles and mechanochemical inference limits.
- Use competing mechanical predictions to identify unresolved parameters and propose discriminating comparisons under stated model assumptions.

## Current limitations

The schedule above is proposed, not measured, and has not been reviewed by a qualified instructor. There are no graded homework sets, midterm or final; the optional project guide and its rehearsal are ungraded practice, and the project draft remains inactive. The four former compact readings now have expanded primers, and the size-scaling reading extends Week 3 without a new week; broader workload, assessment, laboratory, and review gaps remain. The new foundations credit the classical Helfrich and Bell bibliographic records;NCBI metadata was verified but full paper text was not examined. Selected model equations are stated self-contained. Earlier quoted property scales are illustrative textbook-level inputs that vary across sources;no new empirical parameter estimate is supplied. All gels, cells, forces, candidate materials and numbers are synthetic; no lesson gives a laboratory protocol or handling procedure, and nothing here says anything about any real cell type, material or measurement. Qualified human review is required before this course could meet the complete-course standard.
