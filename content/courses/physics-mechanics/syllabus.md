# Physics: Mechanics for Biomedical Engineers

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.7.0.** Personal hobby and learning project with substantial AI assistance; no university equivalence or institutional credit.

Original instruction develops vector motion, free-body balances, contact, conservation laws, the center of mass, rotation, damping and material inference using declared models. See the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) for the comparison with MIT OCW 8.01SC and Open Yale Courses PHYS 200. Week 6 connects fixed-mass momentum and impulse to one-dimensional mass-flow momentum balances, ideal rocket recoil and retained-mass capture. An original text free-body diagram includes a verbal description. The preserved impact lesson remains; the centrifuge example is bounded to ideal circular-motion and rigid-body mechanics.

## Prerequisites

Algebra and trigonometry; Calculus I taken concurrently. Manifest relationships remain provisional and establish no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Build a free-body diagram with consistent axes.
- Apply conservation laws and Newton's laws.
- Quantify torque, angular momentum, and rotational energy.
- Check mechanical predictions against units and limiting cases.

## Proposed 14-week schedule

This is planning scope, not measured workload or a reviewed semester. Week 8 groups a center-of-mass, parallel-axis and system-energy reading with the torque and rolling lesson. Weeks 1, 4, 6 and 9 now have expanded instruction on motion variables, force balances, work and energy, fixed- and variable-mass momentum, impulse, torque, stress, strain and small-strain material models. The existing core has substantive instruction; new week-8 foundations derive coupled massive-pulley constraints and sliding-to-rolling contact power. Week 10 pairs the centrifuge circular-motion reading with universal gravitation and circular orbits. Week 14 is a synthetic machine-compliance/orientation lab and revisits the preserved tendon self-assessment case. All activities are public formative practice.

## Assessment policy

There are 24 scheduled reading/activity routes, 143 public practice items and 79 retrieval cards, plus the preserved case and 5 project milestone self-checks. The seven lessons added in version 0.4.0 have six numeric and three conceptual choices each; the four expanded prototype readings retain their two formative items each. The lab includes CSV summaries, numerical compliance correction, structured choices and multiple-select interpretation. Eight preserved numeric items require units; new items request a bare number in an explicit scale. Significant figures are checked in project rehearsal and new depth-checkpoint fields; dimensions, drawings, written arguments and uncertainty analyses are not automatically scored. An [inactive, ungraded fictional-rotor portfolio draft](assessment-specs/fictional-rotor-tradeoff-project-draft.md) is provided for instructor review and is not activated in the course platform. The optional [rotor trade-off project guide](assignments/rotor-tradeoff-project-guide.md) makes the same project usable as open practice in weeks 11–12, with separate rehearsal candidates, open practice items and five Case studio milestone self-checks whose responses can be saved privately and are not submitted for grading. Unlimited retries provide study feedback, not course grades. Outcome links still need qualified review.

## Connected reports and simulation coursework (open, ungraded)

Four assignments connect collision/capture/frame and energy ledgers (week 7), coupled constraints/forces/torque and pre-impact energy (week 9), phase-resolved slip-to-roll contact simulation (week 10), and oscillator method/stability/dissipation verification (week 14). The last includes an original analytic-reference CSV, explicitly not measured observations. Browser checkpoints assess selected answers, not reports, free-body diagrams or source code. The existing lab and rotor project remain available; actual workload is unknown.

## Current limitations

One data lab is not a laboratory sequence; graded homework, exams and an active graded project remain absent. The optional project guide and its rehearsal are ungraded practice, and the rotor portfolio itself is an inactive instructor-review draft, not an operating specification or equipment recommendation. The centrifuge lesson does not predict sedimentation time or recommend speed, time or temperature. Workload, accessibility and mechanical quality await review. The synthetic lab uses an ideal reference and a supplied linear series model; it does not validate equipment, tissue measurements or unique material mechanisms. Preserved impact approximations are supplemented by a new explicit net/contact force balance, without changing their public questions or keys. A new variable-mass lesson uses a one-dimensional point-body balance with uniform stream velocities and stipulated external force; it omits pressure thrust, engine thermodynamics, multidimensional flow and propulsion design. The package remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only. Resistive forces and gyroscopes, named only by MIT 8.01SC, and waves and fluid statics, named only by Open Yale PHYS 200, remain recorded gaps; thermodynamics is taught in the Thermodynamics & Transport course and special relativity is outside the package.
