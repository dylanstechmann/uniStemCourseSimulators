# Physics: Mechanics for Biomedical Engineers

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.5.0.** Personal hobby and learning project with substantial AI assistance; no university equivalence or institutional credit.

Original instruction develops vector motion, free-body balances, contact, conservation laws, rotation, damping and material inference using declared models. Week 6 connects fixed-mass momentum and impulse to one-dimensional mass-flow momentum balances, ideal rocket recoil and retained-mass capture. An original text free-body diagram includes a verbal description. The preserved impact lesson remains; the centrifuge example is bounded to ideal circular-motion and rigid-body mechanics.

## Prerequisites

Algebra and trigonometry; Calculus I taken concurrently. Manifest relationships remain provisional and establish no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Build a free-body diagram with consistent axes.
- Apply conservation laws and Newton's laws.
- Quantify torque, angular momentum, and rotational energy.
- Check mechanical predictions against units and limiting cases.

## Assessment policy

There are 17 scheduled activities (15 readings, one synthetic data lab and an optional project guide), 114 public practice items and 59 retrieval cards, plus the preserved case and 5 project milestone self-checks. The seven lessons added in version 0.4.0 have six numeric and three conceptual choices each; the four expanded prototype readings retain their two formative items each. The lab includes CSV summaries, numerical compliance correction, structured choices and multiple-select interpretation. Eight preserved numeric items require units; new items request a bare number in an explicit scale. Significant figures are checked only in the project rehearsal's data-interpretation fields; dimensions, drawings, written arguments and uncertainty analyses are not automatically scored. An [inactive, ungraded fictional-rotor portfolio draft](assessment-specs/fictional-rotor-tradeoff-project-draft.md) is provided for instructor review and is not activated in the course platform. The optional [rotor trade-off project guide](assignments/rotor-tradeoff-project-guide.md) makes the same project usable as open practice in weeks 11–12, with separate rehearsal candidates, open practice items and five Case studio milestone self-checks whose responses are saved privately and never submitted or scored. Unlimited retries provide study feedback, not course grades. Outcome links still need qualified review.

## Current limitations

One data lab is not a laboratory sequence; graded homework, exams and an active graded project remain absent. The optional project guide and its rehearsal are ungraded practice, and the rotor portfolio itself is an inactive instructor-review draft, not an operating specification or equipment recommendation. The centrifuge lesson does not predict sedimentation time or recommend speed, time or temperature. Workload, accessibility and mechanical quality await review. The synthetic lab uses an ideal reference and a supplied linear series model; it does not validate equipment, tissue measurements or unique material mechanisms. Preserved impact approximations are supplemented by a new explicit net/contact force balance, without changing their public questions or keys. A new variable-mass lesson uses a one-dimensional point-body balance with uniform stream velocities and stipulated external force; it omits pressure thrust, engine thermodynamics, multidimensional flow and propulsion design. The package remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only.
