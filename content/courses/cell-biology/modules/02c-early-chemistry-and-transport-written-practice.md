# Weeks 1–2 written-response rehearsal: chemistry and transport

Use this optional rehearsal after the molecular-structure, pH, protein, membrane, and transport lessons. It asks for brief explanations in your own words, then gives a self-check. The prompts are not question-bank items: no response is collected, scored, or graded.

## Prompt 1: connect pH and protonation

A monoprotic weak-acid group has a stated pKa. Compare its protonated and deprotonated forms when the surrounding pH equals the pKa and when the pH is one unit above it. State the ratio in each condition, then name one reason the calculation may not exactly describe an ionizable group inside a protein.

**Self-check:** In the ideal Henderson–Hasselbalch model, pH = pKa gives equal protonated and deprotonated populations. One pH unit above pKa gives a 10:1 deprotonated-to-protonated ratio, so about 10/11 (91%) is deprotonated. The relationship is a model for the stated group and conditions. A protein’s local charge, solvent exposure, neighboring groups, and environment can shift its effective behavior; a sequence or pH value alone does not measure that local state.

## Prompt 2: separate equilibrium from transport rate

For potassium at 37 °C, use 5 mM outside and 140 mM inside to interpret the course’s ideal equilibrium-potential estimate, approximately −89 mV (inside relative to outside). Explain what opening a selective potassium channel predicts if the membrane potential is exactly at that value, and what it does not tell you about the cell’s total membrane voltage or the speed of potassium movement.

**Self-check:** At this idealized equilibrium potential, the electrical and chemical contributions for potassium balance, so its net flux is zero even though individual ions still move. A channel provides a passive route and can change the rate of approach to equilibrium; it does not set the equilibrium potential or consume ATP per ion. Other permeant ions, pumps, channel gating, and membrane properties affect the cell and the observed rate.

## Prompt 3: distinguish a route from a measured outcome

A solute enters cells while its concentration is higher outside. Uptake approaches a plateau as the outside concentration rises, and the measured uptake is similar with and without an ATP-synthesis inhibitor. Write one interpretation consistent with facilitated diffusion and two alternatives or limits. Name a follow-up that would test whether movement is passive through the proposed protein rather than an indirect consequence of the treatment.

**Self-check:** The pattern is consistent with a saturable, energy-independent transport process down the solute’s gradient, including carrier-mediated facilitated diffusion. It does not identify a unique carrier: another transport route, a changing gradient, limited assay range, or indirect treatment effects could contribute. A follow-up could compare matched cells with the candidate protein selectively reduced and rescued, verify surface localization and cell integrity, measure both sides of the gradient, and use a vehicle control. ATP-inhibitor results alone do not establish transport mechanism because ATP depletion can alter many cellular processes.

## How to use the rehearsal

Write a few sentences for each prompt before reading its self-check. Underline the measured or calculated quantity, circle the assumption that matters most, and finish with one sentence that states the evidence boundary. If your explanation uses “proves,” revise it to name the observation and the unresolved alternative. These are private study notes, not submitted work.
