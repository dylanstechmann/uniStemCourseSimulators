# Matrix-mechanics design practice

This ungraded exercise supplements the week-12 lesson on matrix mechanics and mechanotransduction. It gives learners a chance to write and self-check an experimental design; the platform does not collect, score, or store a response.

## Design prompt

Two hydrated matrix formulations have similar reported small-strain elastic moduli, but their stress-relaxation measurements differ. Cells on the formulations also differ in nuclear YAP localization and a traction-force index. The coating solutions started with the same fibronectin concentration, but accessible ligand at the cell surface was not measured. Write a short follow-up design that distinguishes accessible ligand, time-dependent matrix mechanics, and force transmission as explanations for the readouts.

In your plan, name:

1. The matrix comparison and the measurements that verify its mechanical and ligand properties.
2. The independent biological unit and how conditions will be assigned.
3. One force-transmission perturbation, with a matched control and a target-engagement check.
4. Direct cellular readouts and a viability or attachment check.
5. The narrowest conclusion that the proposed results could support.

## Self-check

- Measure modulus and stress relaxation in the hydrated culture state, and quantify accessible ligand rather than relying on the starting coating solution.
- Keep cell source/state, passage, density, medium, and timing comparable; randomize conditions within independent culture batches and retain those batches as the replicate units.
- Perturb one prespecified force-transmission component with a matched vehicle or non-targeting control, verify target engagement, and use an orthogonal perturbation or rescue where feasible. Check attachment and viability so cell loss is not mistaken for a signaling effect.
- Treat nuclear localization and traction as different readouts. A pathway perturbation that changes either supports only a contribution under the tested conditions; it does not show a universal matrix response or a change in cell fate.

This is a self-assessed reading exercise. No response is collected or scored, and more than one design can be defensible when it tests the stated alternatives and reports its limits.
