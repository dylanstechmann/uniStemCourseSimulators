## Design prompt

In the lesson's explicitly synthetic matrix-by-cue example, the candidate-marker-positive fraction is highest in the 30 kPa, cue-present condition. A project team describes this result as proof that the cue and matrix produced a functional cell type suitable for tissue repair.

Write a concise follow-up plan that tests the measured lineage-associated result separately from a defined function claim. Use the prompts below as a response template; short phrases or a diagram are acceptable.

1. **Question and claim.** State one testable question and the narrowest claim the existing table supports. Name one stronger claim it does not establish.
2. **Design and unit.** Preserve the two-by-two matrix-modulus-by-cue comparison. Identify the independent differentiation batch as the biological replicate, explain how you would block or randomize conditions within batches, say how technical wells or fields will be summarized, and justify the number of batches using a planned precision or power target rather than the number of cells.
3. **Control the inputs.** Explain how you would verify hydrated matrix mechanics and accessible ligand, keep cell starting state and exposure timing comparable, and verify target engagement if you make a pathway-specific claim.
4. **Separate outcomes.** Name a prespecified, controlled marker measurement and an orthogonal lineage-identity measure. Separately name a defined function assay tied to the proposed cell role; include viable-cell recovery and assay-specific positive and negative controls.
5. **Interpretation and next step.** Describe what you would report if the marker panel changes but the function assay does not. Identify one remaining alternative or scope limit, and the next experiment needed before making a tissue-repair or clinical claim.

## Self-check

- The table supports a descriptive marker-fraction pattern in one synthetic model and time window. It does not prove a stable lineage, mature function, or a treatment benefit.
- The four matrix-by-cue groups remain visible in the design. Independent differentiation batches, not cells or technical wells, are the biological replication units; repeated measurements within a batch do not create extra independent batches.
- The number of independent batches is justified against a prespecified precision or power goal. The sample-size rationale does not count cells, fields, or technical wells as independent units.
- The plan checks the properties cells actually encounter, including hydrated mechanics and accessible ligand, rather than relying only on formulation labels or starting coating concentration. Starting cell state, timing, and handling are controlled or recorded.
- Marker identity and function have distinct, prespecified measurements and controls. A functional assay is interpreted with its calibration, viable-cell denominator, and relevant positive and negative controls.
- If marker evidence changes without the defined function, the report keeps that difference visible and does not call the functional claim successful. A pathway or matrix association remains bounded to the tested model unless a design supports broader inference.
- No in-vitro result in this exercise establishes clinical readiness. A real translational study would need additional scientific, safety, ethical, institutional, and regulatory review appropriate to its setting.

More than one follow-up can be defensible. Compare each plan with the self-check and state what its measurements can and cannot resolve. No points or grade are assigned.

## Additional design exercises

### 1. Variant mechanism and independent controls

One edited cell clone has less Protein X per viable cell and lower total enzyme activity than its mock-edited comparator. RNA abundance is similar, but specific activity per measured Protein X, folding, and localization have not been measured. The result comes from one clone per condition.

Design a follow-up that tests whether the observed difference is reproducibly associated with the variant and separates reduced protein abundance from reduced activity per protein molecule. Include independent clones or reciprocal correction, edit and sequence checks, a validated protein-abundance denominator, an orthogonal functional measurement, and a cell-state or viability check. Predict one result pattern for an abundance mechanism and one for an activity-per-protein mechanism.

**Self-check:** Independent clones and an appropriate mock-edited control address clone and editing effects; sequence confirmation checks the intended change. Measure total activity and Protein X abundance in validated ranges, then interpret their ratio only when both measurements are reliable. A specific-activity difference would support a functional difference under the tested assay conditions, not a unique structural mechanism. Correction or rescue supports reversibility in this cell model but does not establish organism-level causality.

### 2. Distinguish signaling feedback from exposure and receptor effects

After a ligand pulse, phospho-ERK rises and later returns toward baseline. A second pulse produces a smaller response. The original experiment did not measure remaining ligand, surface receptor, or a candidate feedback regulator.

Design a time-resolved follow-up that distinguishes ligand depletion, receptor adaptation, and intracellular negative feedback. State what you would measure during the first response and before and after the second challenge. Include a comparison that tests fresh ligand or controlled medium replacement, a measure of receptor availability, and a justified perturbation of one candidate feedback step with target-engagement evidence. Name a result that would remain ambiguous.

**Self-check:** Sample the same signaling readout at matched early and late times and normalize phospho-ERK to total ERK with time-matched vehicle controls. Fresh ligand or medium exchange tests exposure depletion only if the exchange itself has a matched control. Surface-receptor or occupancy measurements address receptor availability; a selective feedback perturbation needs evidence that its target changed. A smaller second response alone cannot distinguish these mechanisms or establish a downstream cell-fate effect.

### 3. Separate matrix stiffness from presentation and cell-state alternatives

Cells on a nominally stiffer matrix show greater spreading and higher phospho-FAK. The matrices were prepared on different days, accessible ligand was not measured, and the cells came from cultures with different passage histories.

Design a comparison that tests a matrix-mechanics effect while separating it from ligand presentation, matrix relaxation, and starting cell state. Identify the biological replicate, how conditions would be paired or randomized, what matrix properties and cell inputs would be checked, and one proximal signaling plus one functional readout. Explain what an integrin or contractility perturbation could add and why it would not identify the whole mechanism by itself.

**Self-check:** Compare independently prepared matrix batches and independent cell preparations; technical wells or image fields do not add biological replication. Measure hydrated mechanics and accessible ligand rather than relying only on formulation labels, and record relevant cell-state and passage variables. Normalize phospho-FAK to total FAK and include a viable-cell measure. A perturbation can test whether a pathway is required in this setup only when target engagement and appropriate controls are shown; it does not eliminate all matrix or cell-state alternatives.

### 4. Distinguish reduced proliferation, persistent arrest, death, and cell loss

After a treatment, a culture has fewer recovered cells, a lower EdU-positive fraction, and more senescence-associated β-galactosidase staining. The measurements were taken at one time point, with no post-treatment recovery period.

Design a follow-up that can distinguish temporary slowing, persistent senescence-associated arrest, apoptosis, and poor viable-cell recovery. Include a time course, a defined washout or recovery comparison, an orthogonal cell-death measure, more than one senescence-associated feature, and a prespecified denominator for each fraction. Name positive and negative assay controls and state what evidence would still be needed before calling the cells irreversibly senescent.

**Self-check:** Track viable-cell number and recovery as well as fractions; a reduced EdU-positive fraction alone does not identify why proliferation changed. Pair DNA-synthesis measurements with an orthogonal death readout and multiple senescence-associated features, while recognizing that no single marker is definitive. A recovery or re-entry test addresses persistence but depends on cell survival and assay conditions. Keep apoptosis, marker expression, and viable-cell loss as distinct outcomes and avoid calling one a complete cell-state diagnosis.
