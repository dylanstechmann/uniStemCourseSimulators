# Foundations of Cell and Molecular Biology

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.61.1.**

This original course package develops mechanistic reasoning from molecular structure to cell behavior. It is still not a completed 14-week university course: weeks 1–7 have two original topic lessons each, while weeks 4 and 7 retain short prototype readings in addition; weeks 5, 11 and 14 include three original virtual data-analysis labs; week 2 offers an optional written-response rehearsal and week 8 has two synthesis readings plus an ungraded cumulative practice set; weeks 9–14 have original lesson pairs and formative practice. No semester equivalency, university credit, or prerequisite equivalency is claimed.

See the [topic-level scope crosswalk](comparator-scope-crosswalk.md) for a week-by-week comparison with selected public MIT OpenCourseWare topics and explicit coverage boundaries. It is a planning aid, not evidence of equivalency or qualified review.

Use the [independent review handoff](independent-review-handoff.md) to record qualified scientific, assessment, accessibility, workload, and release decisions. No such review or release has occurred.

## Prerequisites

Required background is high-school biology and chemistry, including atoms, simple ions, chemical bonds, concentration, and introductory cell structure. Algebra is used in worked examples. A learner may review unfamiliar chemistry first; the platform does not enforce institutional prerequisites. The course is one foundational component in the repository's public regenerative/stem-cell knowledge map, not evidence of eligibility for any program.

## Intended learning outcomes

By the end of a fully developed version, a learner should be able to:

1. Relate molecular structure, chemical environment, and interactions to plausible biological function while stating what a structure alone cannot establish.
2. Use quantitative models of spatial measurement, membranes, transport, enzyme kinetics, and free-energy change while checking assumptions, units, and limits.
3. Trace genetic information through replication, RNA processing, gene regulation, translation, and phenotype, including context-dependent exceptions.
4. Interpret cell and molecular experiments by identifying the perturbation, measurement, controls, uncertainty, alternative explanations, and scope of a causal claim.

These are intended outcomes. Current assessment coverage is incomplete and does not demonstrate that learners have mastered them.

## Proposed 14-week scope

The course offers a required cell and molecular biology foundation with selected optional applications. Core instruction covers molecular interactions, proteins and membranes, organelles, enzyme energetics, measurement, DNA replication/repair, gene expression, RNA/protein layers, inheritance, signaling, and cell-cycle control/mitosis. Optional extensions are mechanobiology in week 12, senescence/apoptosis in week 13, and developmental potency/regenerative study design in week 14. Learners may skip those applications when following a narrower introductory cell/molecular sequence; the associated homework, lab and studio are ungraded practice.

MIT OCW [7.016 Introductory Biology, Fall 2018 calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-016-introductory-biology-fall-2018/pages/calendar/) is an undergraduate breadth comparator with 36 lectures. It overlaps core biochemical, molecular, genetic and cell topics, and also schedules model organisms, recombinant DNA, human genetics, neurons, cancer, immunology and infectious disease. Those broader introductory-biology areas are outside this local course; the selected optional applications provide a different specialized emphasis. The local 14-week proposal is not an equivalence claim, and its workload is unmeasured. The [objective-to-assessment crosswalk](assessment-crosswalk.md) records the proposed outcomes and current evidence.

Week 5 includes a virtual fluorescence-count data lab with synthetic observations and interactive, ungraded CSV, plotting and evidence checks (unvalidated author-estimated time: 2–3 hours). Week 11 includes a synthetic signaling time-course lab with an output-table upload, fixed-axis plotting, inhibitor contrast and rescue controls (unvalidated author-estimated time: 2–3 hours). Week 8 offers two ungraded synthesis readings and a 25-question, 44-point cumulative practice set; it is not a midterm. The [misconception catalog](misconceptions.md) and [feedback templates](feedback-templates.md) cover selected topics but do not diagnose free-form reasoning.

## Assessment and study policy

All delivered items are public, ungraded formative practice; the course has no weighted grade, deadlines, university credit, midterm, final or active graded project. The eight homework companions and three synthetic-data labs are optional practice with public answer specifications. Week 8 is a cumulative review of weeks 1–7; the week-2 rehearsal and week-12/14 design prompts are self-checks whose written responses are not collected or scored. Midterm, final and project candidate drafts remain inactive, with restricted keys outside the public package and review gates open. The detailed weekly counts and planned mappings remain in the table and crosswalk.
