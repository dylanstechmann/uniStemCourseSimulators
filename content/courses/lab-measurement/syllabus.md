# Laboratory Measurement and Solution Preparation

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.1.0.**

Prepare solutions and dilution series, measure volume and mass with checked instruments, calibrate and use pH meters and spectrophotometers, and rehearse real bench procedures before doing them in a laboratory. This is layer 0 of the wet-lab practice track described in the repository's wet-lab practice plan: the measurement skills every later laboratory package assumes.

This package was written with substantial AI assistance. It has not been reviewed by a laboratory instructor or any certifying body, and it confers no credit, competency record or certification.

## Prerequisites

General Chemistry I recommended; algebra and the mole concept are assumed. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Prepare solutions and dilution series at stated concentrations and back-calculate concentrations from diluted measurements.
- Measure volume and mass with checked instruments, and quantify accuracy and precision from replicate measurements.
- Calibrate and use a pH meter and a spectrophotometer, prepare buffers, and build and use standard curves.
- Connect bench technique to measured results by rehearsing procedures and debriefing their errors.

## Bench practice

The bench simulations replay a procedure from your own choices of action, with real volumes, temperatures and times, hidden faults that change from run to run, and a debrief of every checkpoint once the run ends. The plate-count procedure is adapted from FDA BAM Chapter 3 (January 2026 edition). The pipette check uses a water-density formula from Tanaka and colleagues (2001), with acceptance limits that are practice values. The simulations are formative and cannot train hand skill.

## Optional qualification project (ungraded)

The [project guide](assignments/reagent-and-assay-qualification-project-guide.md) joins the six lessons: plan a HEPES buffer, check a pipette and a balance, calibrate a pH meter, choose the usable range of a curving protein standard curve and report two samples. Four rehearsal items with different numbers sit in week 6, and five Case studio milestones give zero-point self-checks. The data are synthetic.

## Assessment and study policy

Every lesson has public formative items (numeric, single-choice and multiple-select) mapped to its objectives, plus retrieval cards for the review queue. One zero-point case-studio prompt asks for a preparation and verification plan. Nothing is graded.

## Current limitations

The schedule is proposed and unmeasured. Acceptance limits for instruments are examples, and all data are synthetic. Hand skill, aseptic technique and biosafety are not taught here; biosafety and cell culture are later layers of the track.
