## Proposed coverage

1. **Protein structure and binding:** calculate occupancy in a stated 1:1 model and interpret affinity.
2. **Kinetics and inhibition:** estimate apparent parameters from initial-rate observations and interpret a simple inhibitor pattern.
3. **Purification:** calculate activity recovery and specific-activity enrichment from a synthetic preparation.
4. **Free energy and coupling:** add reaction free energies and distinguish coupling from catalysis.
5. **Glycolysis and redox:** account for a stipulated glucose-to-lactate boundary and the NADH/NAD+ role of lactate dehydrogenase.
6. **Citric-acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation:** calculate ATP equivalents under explicit product and P/O assumptions and identify the terminal electron acceptor.
7. **Glycogen and pentose phosphate pathway:** calculate oxidative-branch NADPH and identify a glycogen-phosphorylase product.
8. **Fatty-acid oxidation and ketones:** calculate an integrated palmitate yield under declared beta-oxidation, TCA and P/O assumptions, and identify the hepatic ketolysis limitation.
9. **Fatty-acid synthesis and neutral lipids:** track the NADPH requirement for palmitate and products of complete TAG hydrolysis.
10. **Amino-acid nitrogen and urea cycle:** calculate urea formation and high-energy phosphate equivalents under a stated boundary.
11. **Cholesterol and lipoproteins:** interpret low-sterol SREBP feedback and LDL-receptor recognition.
12. **Nucleotides:** distinguish purine assembly on PRPP from salvage and ribonucleotide reduction.
13. **Protein purification:** select a size-based step after ion exchange and distinguish enrichment from proof of homogeneity.

14. **Cholesterol balance:** calculate a synthetic hepatocyte pool change from synthesis, LDL uptake, bile-acid conversion and VLDL export, then bound the inference relative to plasma LDL.
15. **Purine route accounting:** calculate PRPP use across de novo synthesis and salvage, then keep that ledger separate from GTP and ATP consumed at the IMP branch point.
16. **ATP pool balance:** calculate net ATP-pool amount change from synthetic synthesis/use rates and distinguish a stable amount from ongoing turnover.
17. **Glycolysis and uncoupling:** track pyruvate, ATP and NADH at a glycolysis boundary, then distinguish oxygen use from ATP synthesis in an uncoupled preparation.
18. **Ketone oxidation and hepatic fate:** calculate a conditional acetoacetate yield under explicit TCA/P/O/SCOT assumptions, then bound mouse hepatic isotope evidence to anabolic use rather than canonical oxidative ATP production.
19. **Citric-acid-cycle isotope interpretation:** calculate the change in a synthetic labeled citrate fraction and distinguish enrichment from a quantitative pathway-flux estimate.
20. **Gluconeogenesis:** calculate ATP plus GTP use per glucose and distinguish its complete cofactor cost from the mammalian carbon-source limit on even-chain fatty-acid oxidation.
21. **Citric-acid-cycle pool and flux:** calculate anaplerotic replenishment minus cataplerotic withdrawal, then interpret redox and energy-state ratios without equating pool change to cycle flux.
22. **Glycolysis regulation:** calculate a measured glucose-uptake change and interpret ATP/AMP control of PFK-1 while distinguishing a fructose-6-phosphate pool from glycolytic flux.
23. **Protein structure hierarchy:** distinguish the direct sequence change caused by a substitution from evidence about higher-order folding, and identify the subunit level lost on tetramer dissociation.
24. **Redox free energy:** calculate ΔG°′ from a stated electron-transfer potential difference and distinguish energy release in the respiratory chain from proton-gradient-driven ATP synthesis.
25. **Ligand depletion:** use the exact quadratic binding solution and compare it with the hyperbolic approximation when receptor concentration is not negligible.
26. **Uncoupling and ATP turnover:** calculate the ATP rate implied by oxygen uptake and a stated P/O yield, then distinguish higher oxygen use from higher ATP turnover when coupling falls.
27. **Flux control coefficients:** use the summation theorem to find an unreported coefficient and estimate the small flux response to an enzyme perturbation.

## Additional integrative cases

**Case 28 — redox and carbon-source evidence.** Use the synthetic lactate/pyruvate concentrations to interpret the LDH-linked redox shift at fixed pH. Correct the lactate M+3 fraction for medium glucose enrichment to estimate the glucose-derived share, and explain why that fraction is not a rate.


**Case 29 — ATP demand and diagnostic oxygen use.** Calculate the glucose uptake needed when oxidative phosphorylation is fully blocked, then use the paired oxygen-consumption results to distinguish the modeled respiratory-chain and glycolytic defects. Include the expected lactate output and one reason to corroborate the oxygen measurement.


**Case 30 — enzyme abundance and regulatory time scales.** Calculate the time for a first-order enzyme-amount change to complete 90% of its transition after a synthesis-rate shift, then compare that slow abundance response with allosteric and covalent control.


**Case 31 — cooperative oxygen delivery.** Use the Hill equation to calculate oxygen unloading between lung and resting-tissue pressures, compare hemoglobin-like and myoglobin-like carriers, and explain what the Hill coefficient represents.


**Case 32 — pool size and steady-state flux.** Compare two perturbations of a two-step first-order pathway that give the same intermediate pool. Calculate their flux difference and explain why pool concentration alone cannot determine flow.


**Case 33 — fractional protonation and net charge.** Use the acid and base fractional-charge equations to sum the expected charge of a synthetic short peptide at a stated pH, then explain the independent-group approximation.


**Case 34 — isoelectric-point pKa bracketing.** Calculate the pI of an amino acid without an ionizable side chain, then identify the pKa pairs that bracket the neutral form for acidic and basic side chains.


**Case 35 — protein charge and separation behavior.** Calculate how a synthetic protein’s net charge changes with pH, then use pH relative to pI to predict migration direction and the ion-exchange resin that binds it.


**Case 36 — catalytic efficiency and substrate regime.** Convert Km to molar units, calculate kcat/Km, and compare low-substrate behavior with the near-saturation limit without treating efficiency as binding affinity.


**Case 37 — pathway-regulation layers.** Calculate a change in substrate supply and classify controlled perturbations that isolate allosteric, covalent-modification and enzyme-expression responses.


**Case 38 — connected-pathway partial block.** Calculate the initial intermediate-pool accumulation after a partial pathway block, predict the connected downstream response, and distinguish the initial model from later compensation.


**Case 39 — two-state folding equilibrium.** Convert unfolding free energy to an equilibrium constant and folded fraction, keeping the sign convention and two-state assumption explicit.

**Case 40 — denaturant stability and midpoint.** Estimate ΔG_H₂O and the denaturation midpoint from two synthetic unfolding free energies measured at stated urea concentrations, using the linear m-value model.

**Case 41 — mutation, marginal stability and proteostasis.** Calculate how a destabilizing mutation shifts the two-state unfolding equilibrium, then interpret why a much larger unfolded population can matter to chaperone and degradation systems even when most protein remains folded.

**Case 42 — replicate initial-rate summary.** Calculate the mean rate in each of four synthetic condition-by-substrate groups, keeping the control and inhibitor observations separate and retaining n = 3 independent reactions per group.

**Case 43 — kinetic parameters and inhibition constant.** Derive control Vmax and Km, apparent Km under inhibitor, and Ki from synthetic double-reciprocal fit coefficients and a stated inhibitor concentration.

**Case 44 — inhibition evidence and assay limits.** Interpret the fitted parameter pattern and the limits of a reciprocal plot, then select a time interval that meets substrate-depletion and product-inhibition requirements for initial rates.

**Case 45 — pentose demand and oxidative-branch availability.** Use the reversible nonoxidative balance to connect glycolytic intermediates to pentose phosphates, then relate NADP+ availability to oxidative-branch flux without treating cofactor availability as a complete flux predictor.

**Case 46 — amino-acid carbon entry and fate.** Map representative amino-acid carbon skeletons to pyruvate, oxaloacetate, acetyl-CoA/acetoacetate, or fumarate plus acetoacetate, then classify them as glucogenic, ketogenic or both.

**Case 47 — separate nitrogen and carbon fate.** Interpret alanine transamination as distinct nitrogen and carbon ledgers, then explain why acetyl-CoA entry into the citric acid cycle does not support net glucose production in mammals.

**Case 48 — citrate shuttle and malonyl-CoA control.** Explain how citrate export supplies cytosolic acetyl-CoA, then connect ACC regulation and malonyl-CoA production to both fatty-acid synthesis and CPT1 inhibition.

**Case 49 — neutral-lipid storage and VLDL export.** Trace glycerol-3-phosphate acylation through phosphatidic acid and diacylglycerol to triacylglycerol, then connect hepatic TAG storage to VLDL export.

**Case 50 — amino-acid tracer contribution and scope.** Calculate a synthetic cell-model change in BCAA contribution to lipogenic acetyl-CoA, then distinguish that pool-specific assignment from total oxidation, net glucose production and whole-body inference.

**Case 51 — labeled ribose and flux limits.** Calculate a synthetic M+1 plus M+2 ribose fraction, then distinguish label appearance from assignment of pathway flux.


**Case 52 — urea-cycle nitrogen and energy ledger.** Convert a synthetic 5 mol nitrogen-atom load into urea under complete closed-balance disposal, then distinguish the resulting 7.50 mol ATP-molecule demand from 10 mol high-energy phosphate equivalents.
