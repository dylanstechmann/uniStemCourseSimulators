# Biochemistry I: proposed grading policy candidate

**Status: inactive proposal for review only.** This document does not change the course's formative-only manifest, assign work, create deadlines, or award a grade. The category weights and rules below are author proposals, not an institutional policy. They require instructor, assessment, accessibility, workload and institutional review before any use.

## Proposed category weights

| Category | Proposed course weight | Components |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Homework | 40% | Eight homework candidates, weighted equally at 5% each. Normalize each packet to a percentage before averaging so different raw point totals do not change its course weight. |
| Midterm | 20% | One first-half exam candidate covering proposed weeks 1–7. |
| Cumulative final | 25% | One cumulative exam candidate covering the proposed course. |
| Integrative project | 15% | One human-scored protein-state and ligand-binding portfolio, scored with the separate five-criterion rubric. |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

The two virtual-data labs, public lesson questions, retrieval cards and week-14 case remain formative and do not enter this proposed total. The labs use supplied synthetic observations and do not assess physical laboratory technique. The project rubric is uncalibrated; no summative component is approved.

## Proposed calculation and grade bands

Let each homework percentage be its earned points divided by its available points. The proposed final percentage is `0.40 × mean(homework percentages) + 0.20 × midterm percentage + 0.25 × final percentage + 0.15 × project percentage`. The human-scored project percentage is the rubric total out of 100. Keep full precision during calculation and round only the final percentage to two decimal places using half-up rounding.

Proposed letter bands are A: 90.00–100.00; B: 80.00–89.99; C: 70.00–79.99; D: 60.00–69.99; and F: below 60.00. Do not curve scores. These bands are illustrative and require institutional approval; they establish no credit equivalency or program requirement.

## Proposed attempts, feedback and submission rules

- Homework: allow up to two recorded attempts per packet and count the higher score. Provide item-level feedback after each attempt; withhold protected keys until the packet closes.
- Midterm and final: allow one scored attempt in a published window. Provide feedback after that exam window closes. Any approved make-up requires a separately reviewed protected form.
- Project: accept one portfolio submission. Return criterion-level scores and notes. Before use, calibrate the rubric against sample work and define a revision or re-evaluation path; neither is currently approved.
- Publish dated due and exam windows before enrollment. The 14-week sequence currently labels weeks only and supplies no calendar dates.
- Proposed homework and project grace period: 72 hours after the published deadline without penalty. Beyond that period, an instructor-approved extension or accommodation must set a revised deadline. Exams require a make-up window rather than a late upload.
- An unsubmitted item counts as zero only after its applicable deadline or approved extension expires. Work under an approved extension remains pending until that date.
- Learners may request a review within seven calendar days after a score and its feedback are released. Preserve the original score and record any adjustment and rationale. A project review should include a second qualified scorer; an exam review must use the exact saved form and source version.

## Access, transparency and academic integrity

Before use, provide accessible digital text, semantic alternatives for equations, keyboard-operable answer entry, screen-reader-readable tables, and equivalent CSV instructions. Apply approved accommodations to time limits, deadlines and format without reducing the learning outcome being assessed. Publish what collaboration, calculators, references and AI-assisted tools are permitted before each assignment opens; this package does not yet set those rules.

Explain each category weight, raw-to-percent conversion, attempt rule, feedback-release date and grade band to learners before enrollment. Provide an itemized grade calculation and a route to report arithmetic, accessibility or grading errors. Do not use the proposed total as evidence of laboratory competence, university credit, prerequisite equivalency or professional qualification.

## Review and implementation gates

Before adoption, a qualified instructor must approve the weights, grade bands, due-date calendar, attempt limits, missing-work rule, extension process and integrity statement. Reviewers must inspect the assessment blueprint, independently verify representative keys and grader behavior, evaluate accessibility and fairness, and calibrate the human rubric on sample work. Measure learner workload and revise the proposed schedule. Confirm protected exam forms, exact-version grade calculations, appeals and accommodation workflows in the application. Record institutional scope and operator release decisions against a frozen package digest.

Until those gates are met, `course.json` must keep `grading_policy.mode` set to `formative-only` with no graded categories, and every homework, exam and project candidate remains inactive.
