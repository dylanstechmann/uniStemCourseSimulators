2. Use the listed pKa and pH values to reason about protonation and calculate the isoelectric point for the supplied amino-acid model.


3. Predict the sign of the fictional protein's net charge relative to its pI and choose a charge-based ion-exchange condition consistent with the stated pH.


4. Trace urea's immediate atom donors and account for ATP use, then relate nitrogen transfer and compartmentation to the pathway's connection with central metabolism.


5. Classify the listed carbon skeletons and explain the distinction between removing amino nitrogen and the subsequent carbon fate.


6. Apply the stated biochemical boundary to decide whether acetyl-CoA alone supports net glucose synthesis in mammals, and identify what additional pathway entry would be needed for a different carbon source.
7. Calculate the percentage-point change in a synthetic BCAA contribution to lipogenic acetyl-CoA and distinguish the cell-model result from claims about leucine classification or whole-body human flux.
8. Use a synthetic nitrogen-atom disposal load to calculate urea amount and high-energy phosphate demand under an explicit closed-balance assumption.
