# Biochemistry I: Proteins, Enzymes & Metabolism

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.5.1.**

A mechanistic and quantitative introduction to protein structure, enzyme kinetics, thermodynamics, central metabolism, and pathway regulation.

## Prerequisites

General Chemistry II; Organic Chemistry I recommended. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Course boundary and comparator

The proposed sequence emphasizes proteins, enzymes and heterotrophic animal-cell metabolism. Photosystem light harvesting, photosynthetic electron transport, photophosphorylation and Calvin–Benson CO₂ assimilation are deliberately deferred; the central-carbon overview does not teach them. Membrane transport mechanisms and translation are also not dedicated strands. This is a material scope difference from MIT OCW [7.05 General Biochemistry, Spring 2020](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-05-general-biochemistry-spring-2020/pages/calendar/): its calendar includes channels, transporters and pumps (lecture 13), photosynthesis and CO₂ assimilation (lectures 26–27), and translation (lectures 17–18). The OCW syllabus notes that its posting covers primarily the second half of the course, with the first half on MITx. MIT 7.05 is a breadth and sequence comparator, not a claim of equivalence. A broader biochemistry sequence should add these topics in dedicated units.

## Authored outcomes

- Interpret protein sequence, structure, and binding models.
- Quantify enzyme saturation and inhibition.
- Track carbon, electrons, and energy through central metabolism.
- Explain pathway control through multiple regulatory layers.
- Evaluate protein separation and purification data to judge selectivity, recovery and enrichment.

## Proposed 14-week schedule

This is a proposed scope, not a measured or reviewed semester. Topics are staged so that an introductory overview leads into one designated quantitative treatment: structure and recognition in week 1 precede binding equilibria in week 4; free-energy and redox tools in week 8 precede their pathway applications in weeks 9–11; and network-level perturbation in week 12 precedes quantitative flux control in week 13. Dedicated pathway lessons carry the detailed glycolysis, citric-acid-cycle, gluconeogenesis and storage/lipid accounting. Every topic lesson has public formative items and retrieval cards; homework, synthesis and project lessons pair with their own open practice sets or self-checks.

## Assessment and study policy

Every topic lesson has public formative items (numeric, single-choice, multiple-select, data-interpretation, structured-response and CSV-upload) mapped to its objectives, plus retrieval cards for the review queue; each practice assessment also lists the course outcomes its items assess. Eight open homework practice sets, a week-7 midterm review practice set, a week-14 cumulative final review practice set and a protein-state project rehearsal follow the same rules; their packets and answer specifications are public, so none is a protected assignment or exam. Weeks 4 and 7 include synthetic-data virtual labs; week 14 embeds its metabolic perturbation case and objective-aligned self-check in the lesson. The virtual labs analyze supplied fictional datasets and provide no experimental protocols. These are practice activities with unlimited retries and no institutional grade, university credit or transferable credit. Case responses are not submitted, collected or graded. Workload has not been measured.

An [inactive grading-policy proposal](assessment-specs/grading-policy-candidate.md) sketches possible weights and learner-facing rules for later review. It does not change the formative-only policy above: the manifest still has no graded categories, deadlines or summative grade.

## Virtual-data lab sequence

The [week-4 purification lab](labs/02-protein-purification-from-fractionation-data.md) uses a synthetic fraction table to examine activity recovery, enrichment and the limits of purity claims. The [week-7 kinetics lab](labs/01-enzyme-kinetics-and-inhibition-from-data.md) uses synthetic replicate rates to estimate kinetic parameters and compare inhibition models. Both are ungraded data-analysis exercises; neither describes a physical laboratory procedure.

## Current limitations

The schedule is proposed, not measured, and has not been reviewed by a qualified instructor. The virtual labs provide a limited synthetic-data analysis sequence, not physical laboratory training. Homework, midterm and final review and project practice is open and ungraded; the homework, exam and project candidates remain inactive, and no summative assessment is approved or graded. Numerical examples use synthetic data and textbook-level values that can vary between sources. No lesson provides a laboratory protocol or medical advice. Qualified human review is required before this package could meet a complete-course standard.
