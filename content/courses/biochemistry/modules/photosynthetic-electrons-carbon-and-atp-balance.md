# Photosynthetic electrons, carbon and ATP balance

## Extend the course beyond animal-cell oxidation

The central-carbon and mitochondrial lessons emphasize heterotrophic metabolism. Photosynthesis adds an energy-conversion and carbon-assimilation direction that cannot be inferred by reversing glycolysis. In oxygenic photosynthesis, light-driven electron transfer links water oxidation to reducing power, and proton-gradient coupling supports ATP synthesis. The Calvin–Benson cycle uses energy and reductant to assimilate inorganic carbon. Light reactions and carbon assimilation are coupled through metabolites and regulation; calling the cycle “dark” does not mean it normally proceeds independently of light-supported energy supply.

The two photosystems have different roles. Photosystem II supplies oxidizing power to extract electrons from water, releasing oxygen and protons into the lumen-side accounting. Electrons pass through carriers including plastoquinone and the cytochrome b6f complex. Photosystem I uses another light-driven excitation before electrons reduce a terminal acceptor pathway leading to NADPH. This two-excitation architecture overcomes an energetic span that one ordinary downhill electron-transfer chain cannot supply. Antenna absorption, transfer losses and regulation mean a minimal photon ledger is not a measured quantum efficiency.

## Specify a linear electron ledger

For four electrons supplied by oxidizing two water molecules, one oxygen molecule is released and two NADPH molecules can be formed in the ideal terminal reduction ledger. At least four productive PSII and four productive PSI excitations are required in this simplified count: eight photons for those four electrons. We omit alternative sinks, charge recombination, absorption losses and maintenance. Each omission belongs beside the output rather than hidden inside a universal yield claim.

For an explicitly stipulated proton ledger, take four lumen protons from water oxidation and eight from the coupled plastoquinone/b6f route per four linear electrons. This totals 12 protons available to the chosen ATP-coupling boundary. Stipulate an ideal effective ATP cost h=14/3 protons per ATP for this exercise, without claiming that it is a universal value for every organism, transport cost or physiological state. The linear ledger then supplies 12/h=18/7 ATP together with two NADPH. Proton leaks and additional transport demands would lower the usable output.

## Carbon fixation and regeneration

Rubisco carboxylation combines a five-carbon acceptor, ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate, with one CO2. The unstable six-carbon intermediate yields two molecules of three-carbon 3-phosphoglycerate. Reduction uses ATP and NADPH to generate triose phosphate. For three CO2 inputs, six triose-phosphate equivalents are formed at that stage; five are reorganized to regenerate three five-carbon acceptors and one is a net three-carbon output.

The standard simplified net ledger for that three-CO2 output requires nine ATP and six NADPH. Six ATP support phosphorylation before reduction and three support regeneration of the acceptor. Carbon counts close as 3×1=1×3 net, while the five retained triose equivalents contain the 15 carbons needed for three five-carbon acceptors. A net triose-phosphate output is not a whole glucose molecule. Export, starch/sucrose synthesis and photorespiration require additional accounting beyond this bounded cycle.

## Match the two ledgers

Supplying six NADPH requires three of the four-electron linear blocks: twelve linear electrons, three oxygen molecules and a minimal 24 productive linear photons. Those blocks supply 36 lumen protons under the stipulated model. Nine ATP at h=14/3 require 42 protons, leaving a six-proton deficit. Thus satisfying the reductant requirement does not automatically satisfy ATP demand.

Suppose a declared cyclic PSI route supplies two additional lumen protons per cyclic electron and one productive PSI photon per such electron, with no net NADPH or oxygen production. Three cyclic electrons supply the missing six protons and add three productive photons, giving a minimum total of 27 photons within this combined ideal model. The cyclic route adjusts the ATP/reductant balance rather than creating additional fixed carbon or terminal reducing power by itself. Its mechanism and stoichiometry are specified assumptions; other routes or costs would require a new ledger.

## Separate worked comparison and limiting resources

For a separate ideal ATP cost of four protons per ATP, the same 36 linear protons supply nine ATP and no cyclic addition is needed for the stated six-NADPH carbon output. This comparison shows why an unstated coupling cost can change a claim about cyclic demand. It does not demonstrate that one real organism is always more efficient than another.

As a rehearsal in a different boundary, take an available pool of 18 ATP and nine NADPH solely for the simplified Calvin ledger. Reductant limits net triose output to 1.5 units, consuming 4.5 CO2 and 13.5 ATP, leaving 4.5 ATP. Fractional outputs are ensemble amounts, not fractional individual molecules in a literal single-cycle event. A carbon-output claim must satisfy both energy and reducing-equivalent constraints.
