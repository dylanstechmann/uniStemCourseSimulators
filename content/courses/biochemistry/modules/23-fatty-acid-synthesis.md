# Fatty-acid synthesis: acetyl-CoA, ATP and NADPH

Fatty-acid synthesis stores excess carbon as a reduced, energy-dense molecule. It uses cytosolic acetyl-CoA, ATP and NADPH, so it connects the citrate shuttle, the pentose phosphate pathway and fatty-acid oxidation. The synthesis and oxidation routes use different compartments and enzymes; their regulation also helps prevent strong simultaneous flux in opposite directions.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Calculate acetyl-CoA, malonyl-CoA, ATP and NADPH requirements for palmitate synthesis from cytosolic acetyl-CoA.
2. Explain citrate export, acetyl-CoA carboxylase regulation and how malonyl-CoA coordinates synthesis with long-chain fatty-acid entry into mitochondria.
3. Trace glycerol-3-phosphate acylation to triacylglycerol and explain how storage connects to liver VLDL export.
4. Compare intracellular adipose lipolysis with capillary lipoprotein lipase and trace the carbon products of stored triacylglycerol mobilization.

## Supply acetyl-CoA to the cytosol

Acetyl-CoA made in the mitochondrial matrix does not cross the inner mitochondrial membrane directly. When carbon and energy are abundant, mitochondrial acetyl units can be condensed with oxaloacetate to form citrate; citrate is transported to the cytosol and ATP-citrate lyase regenerates cytosolic acetyl-CoA. The shuttle also yields oxaloacetate, which is converted through malate and pyruvate in a route that can contribute reducing power via cytosolic NADPH-producing malic enzyme. The shuttle boundary consumes ATP and is separate from the fatty-acid-synthase ledger below.

## Add two-carbon units with ATP and NADPH

Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC) uses ATP, bicarbonate and biotin to convert acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA. Fatty-acid synthase loads one acetyl starter and uses seven malonyl units to build the 16-carbon chain of palmitate. Each elongation cycle adds two net carbons. The reductions use NADPH; forming palmitate from eight cytosolic acetyl-CoA consumes **7 ATP at ACC and 14 NADPH in fatty-acid synthase**, before counting the citrate shuttle or any costs of making the starting acetyl-CoA.

For the carbon count, one acetyl group primes the synthase and seven additional acetyl-CoA molecules are carboxylated to malonyl-CoA. The carboxyl carbons added by ACC are released again during condensation, so eight two-carbon acetyl units yield the 16-carbon fatty-acid chain. Counting seven malonyl units as seven extra two-carbon additions on top of eight acetyl units would overcount the product.

## Coordinate synthesis with oxidation

Citrate can signal carbon abundance and activate ACC in a simplified allosteric model. Phosphorylation by AMP-activated protein kinase inhibits ACC; insulin-linked dephosphorylation and longer-term expression changes can favor synthesis in fed conditions. Exact effects depend on tissue and physiological state. ACC produces malonyl-CoA, which both donates two-carbon units to fatty-acid synthase and inhibits CPT1, limiting long-chain acyl-group entry into mitochondrial beta-oxidation. This links anabolism and catabolism through a shared control signal.

The oxidative pentose phosphate pathway is one source of NADPH for fatty-acid synthesis. NADPH demand can therefore influence glucose 6-phosphate partitioning, while cytosolic acetyl-CoA supply, ATP, citrate, ACC state and product demand also shape synthesis. A single metabolite level does not determine whole-cell flux by itself.

## Store and mobilize triacylglycerol

Fatty acyl-CoA can be stored as triacylglycerol (TAG) or directed into membrane lipids. In the glycerol-3-phosphate route, glycerol-3-phosphate is acylated to lysophosphatidic acid, then phosphatidic acid and diacylglycerol. Diacylglycerol acyltransferase adds the third fatty acyl-CoA to make TAG. Adipocytes can supply glycerol-3-phosphate from glycolysis through dihydroxyacetone phosphate; the liver can also phosphorylate glycerol. Hepatocytes package TAG into VLDL, linking storage-lipid synthesis to the lipoprotein routes in the cholesterol lesson.

During fasting or other energy-demand states, adipocyte lipid droplets release stored fatty acids and glycerol through sequential hydrolysis. ATGL converts TAG to diacylglycerol, hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) converts diacylglycerol to monoacylglycerol, and monoacylglycerol lipase releases glycerol. Each step releases one fatty acid. In a simplified hormonal model, catecholamine signaling through cAMP and protein kinase A changes perilipin and HSL phosphorylation, allowing lipases greater access to the droplet; perilipin-associated CGI-58 can activate ATGL. Insulin generally suppresses adipose lipolysis in the fed state. Tissue-specific regulation and additional lipid-droplet proteins refine this model.

For a synthetic stoichiometric check, complete hydrolysis of 5 mol TAG releases 15 mol fatty acids and 5 mol glycerol. This counts carbon products only, not ATP production.

The three fatty acids can enter circulation bound to albumin or be re-esterified; oxidative tissues can use them through beta-oxidation. Released glycerol commonly travels to liver, where glycerol kinase forms glycerol-3-phosphate and metabolism can continue through dihydroxyacetone phosphate. This carbon can support gluconeogenesis. Intracellular adipose lipolysis is distinct from capillary lipoprotein lipase: LPL hydrolyzes circulating TAG in chylomicrons and VLDL, while ATGL, HSL and MGL mobilize TAG stored inside cells.

## Worked example

**Problem.** A synthetic cytosolic preparation starts with 24 mol acetyl-CoA and uses all of it for palmitate synthesis. How many mol palmitate, malonyl-CoA and ATP are produced or consumed, and how much NADPH is required?

**Step 1: group acetyl units.** Eight mol acetyl-CoA make one mol palmitate, so 24 mol acetyl-CoA form **3 mol palmitate**.

**Step 2: scale the synthesis ledger.** Each palmitate requires 7 mol malonyl-CoA, 7 mol ATP at ACC and 14 mol NADPH at fatty-acid synthase. The preparation therefore forms **21 mol malonyl-CoA**, consumes **21 mol ATP** and **42 mol NADPH**. Cytosolic acetyl-CoA is the starting boundary, so citrate-shuttle costs are excluded.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming mitochondrial acetyl-CoA crosses the inner membrane directly into the cytosol.
- Counting malonyl-CoA's temporary added carbon as part of the final 16-carbon chain.
- Forgetting that ACC costs ATP and fatty-acid synthase consumes NADPH.
- Reporting synthesis cost without saying whether acetyl-CoA supply and the citrate shuttle are inside the boundary.
- Treating NADPH as interchangeable with NADH or as an ATP product.
- Assuming malonyl-CoA activates CPT1; it inhibits long-chain acyl-group entry and therefore opposes beta-oxidation.
- Confusing capillary lipoprotein lipase with the sequential ATGL-HSL-MGL enzymes that mobilize intracellular adipose TAG.
- Counting TAG hydrolysis products as one fatty acid and one glycerol; complete hydrolysis releases three fatty acids per glycerol backbone.
- Assuming adipose tissue and liver obtain glycerol-3-phosphate from identical routes in all conditions.
