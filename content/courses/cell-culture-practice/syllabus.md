# Cell Culture Practice (pilot: Counting Cells and Viability)

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.1.0.**

This package is a one-lesson pilot of layer 2 of the wet-lab practice track. **Only lesson 4 of the eight outlined cell-culture topics, counting cells and viability, is written.** Aseptic technique, media and reagents, passaging, cryopreservation, contamination, human pluripotent stem cells and records are planned but absent; the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) lists each as deferred. Enrolling gives you this one lesson, its practice and the linked bench simulation, not a cell-culture course.

The package was written with substantial AI assistance. It has not been reviewed by a laboratory professional or any certifying body, confers no credit, competency record or qualification, and does not replace the training and supervision your institution requires before you handle cells.

## Prerequisites

Laboratory Measurement and Solution Preparation, Laboratory Safety and Biosafety, and Foundations of Cell and Molecular Biology are recommended. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Estimate viable-cell concentration and viability from hemocytometer counts, accounting for sampling, dilution, chamber geometry and a consistent boundary rule.
- Judge the reliability and meaning of a manual count: counting uncertainty, systematic preparation errors and the limits of dye-exclusion viability.

## Assessment and study policy

Twelve public formative items (numeric, single-choice and multiple-select) cover dilution factors, chamber volume, boundary and stain decisions, concentration and viability, Poisson counting uncertainty, chamber disagreement, systematic error and the limits of trypan blue. Four retrieval cards and one zero-point self-check case accompany them. The bench simulation's nine-checkpoint debrief is formative feedback on your own tallies. Nothing is graded, and there is no project in this pilot.

## Current limitations

The counting rules are taught in text and practised on synthetic, non-human fields; the simulation cannot train focusing, recognising cells and debris, pipetting or loading a real chamber. The top/left boundary convention, loading steps, the simulation's twofold dilution, its 20–80-cell field range and its numerical tolerances are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. Automated counters and fluorescent viability assays are named only.
