# Cell Culture Practice (partial: Aseptic Technique and Counting Cells)

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.2.0.**

This package is a two-lesson partial version of layer 2 of the wet-lab practice track. **Outline lessons 1 and 4 of the eight planned cell-culture topics are written: aseptic technique, and counting cells and viability.** Media and reagents, passaging, cryopreservation, full contamination management and authentication, human pluripotent stem cells and records remain planned but absent; the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) lists each status. Enrolling gives these two lessons, their practice and the linked bench simulations, not a cell-culture course.

The package was written with substantial AI assistance. It has not been reviewed by a laboratory professional or any certifying body, confers no credit, competency record or qualification, and does not replace the training and supervision your institution requires before you handle cells.

## Prerequisites

Laboratory Measurement and Solution Preparation, Laboratory Safety and Biosafety, and Foundations of Cell and Molecular Biology are recommended. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Perform an aseptic medium change in a Class II cabinet using pre-work checks, clean-to-dirty layout and sterile-to-sterile technique.
- Interpret contamination evidence from visible appearance, sterility incubation, filtration and dedicated mycoplasma testing, and evaluate containment and antibiotic decisions.
- Estimate viable-cell concentration and viability from hemocytometer counts, accounting for sampling, dilution, chamber geometry and a consistent boundary rule.
- Judge the reliability and meaning of a manual count: counting uncertainty, systematic preparation errors and the limits of dye-exclusion viability.

## Assessment and study policy

Twenty-four public formative items cover aseptic layout and manipulation, sterility-incubation arithmetic, mycoplasma detection, contamination response, dilution factors, chamber volume, boundary and stain decisions, concentration and viability, Poisson counting uncertainty, chamber disagreement, systematic error and the limits of trypan blue. Eight retrieval cards and two zero-point self-check cases accompany them. The aseptic simulation's 25-checkpoint debrief and the hemocytometer simulation's nine-checkpoint debrief are formative feedback on your own decisions and tallies. Nothing is graded, and there is no project in this partial package.

## Current limitations

The aseptic lesson is taught in text and practised on a synthetic medium change; the simulation cannot train hand skill, physical cabinet ergonomics, real organism identification or institutional contamination response. Its T-75 volume, clean-to-dirty zones, one-bottle rule, hidden-event probabilities, visible-growth threshold and simplified mycoplasma assay are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. FDA process-simulation incubation rules are adapted for sterility-control teaching and do not establish regulatory compliance.

The counting lesson is practised on synthetic, non-human fields; the simulation cannot train focusing, recognising cells and debris, pipetting or loading a real chamber. The top/left boundary convention, loading steps, the simulation's twofold dilution, its 20–80-cell field range and its numerical tolerances are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. Automated counters and fluorescent viability assays are named only.
