# Cell Culture Practice (partial: Aseptic Technique, Media, Passaging and Counting Cells)

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.3.0.**

This package is a four-lesson partial version of layer 2 of the wet-lab practice track. **Outline lessons 1–4 of the eight planned cell-culture topics are written: aseptic technique; media, supplements and reagent preparation; passaging adherent and suspension cells; and counting cells and viability.** Cryopreservation, full contamination management and authentication, human pluripotent stem cells and records remain planned but absent; the [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) lists each status. Enrolling gives these four lessons, their practice and four linked bench simulations, not a cell-culture course.

The package was written with substantial AI assistance. It has not been reviewed by a laboratory professional or any certifying body, confers no credit, competency record or qualification, and does not replace the training and supervision your institution requires before you handle cells.

## Prerequisites

Laboratory Measurement and Solution Preparation, Laboratory Safety and Biosafety, and Foundations of Cell and Molecular Biology are recommended. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Perform an aseptic medium change in a Class II cabinet using pre-work checks, clean-to-dirty layout and sterile-to-sterile technique.
- Interpret contamination evidence from visible appearance, sterility incubation, filtration and dedicated mycoplasma testing, and evaluate containment and antibiotic decisions.
- Estimate viable-cell concentration and viability from hemocytometer counts, accounting for sampling, dilution, chamber geometry and a consistent boundary rule.
- Judge the reliability and meaning of a manual count: counting uncertainty, systematic preparation errors and the limits of dye-exclusion viability.
- Prepare a traceable supplemented-medium batch by calculating stock volumes, evaluating medium formulation, component suitability and QC evidence, and specifying filtration, filter-integrity checks, sterility incubation, dedicated mollicute testing, labelling, storage and release records.
- Plan and document an adherent passage to a specified viable-cell target density, contrast it with suspension passaging by dilution, and interpret split ratios and population doubling level.

## Assessment and study policy

Forty-eight public formative items cover aseptic layout and manipulation, contamination evidence, medium formulation and supplement arithmetic, filtration and QC, passaging decisions, target-density and population-doubling calculations, dilution factors, chamber volume, boundary and stain decisions, concentration and viability, Poisson counting uncertainty, systematic error and the limits of trypan blue. Sixteen retrieval cards and four zero-point self-check cases accompany them. The aseptic simulation's 25-checkpoint debrief, hemocytometer simulation's nine-checkpoint debrief, supplemented-medium simulation's 14-checkpoint debrief and adherent-passage simulation's 16-checkpoint debrief are formative feedback on your own decisions and tallies. Nothing is graded, and there is no project in this partial package.

## Current limitations

The aseptic lesson is taught in text and practised on a synthetic medium change; the simulation cannot train hand skill, physical cabinet ergonomics, real organism identification or institutional contamination response. Its T-75 volume, clean-to-dirty zones, one-bottle rule, hidden-event probabilities, visible-growth threshold and simplified mycoplasma assay are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. FDA process-simulation incubation rules are adapted for sterility-control teaching and do not establish regulatory compliance.

The counting lesson is practised on synthetic, non-human fields; the simulation cannot train focusing, recognising cells and debris, pipetting or loading a real chamber. The top/left boundary convention, loading steps, the simulation's twofold dilution, its 20–80-cell field range and its numerical tolerances are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. Automated counters and fluorescent viability assays are named only.

The media lesson uses a synthetic 500 mL batch, pH 7.10–7.30 and 280–310 mOsm/kg windows, 98% recovery, a 10 mL control, a 14-day expiry and hidden events. These, its simplified assays and volume-weighted osmolality approximation are simulation choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria or physical validation. Real formulations, filter-integrity methods, storage and expiry follow product and institutional procedures.

Lesson 3 covers suspension passaging conceptually but simulates only an adherent T-75 target-density workflow. Its confluence window and refusal scenario, exact flask volumes, dissociation-time bound, viability penalties, target density, PDL convention and numerical tolerances are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. The incubation action records conditions but predicts no attachment, growth, phenotype, sterility or future viability. Neither the reading nor the simulation establishes physical technique, competency or institutional authorisation.
