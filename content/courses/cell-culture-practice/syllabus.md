# Cell Culture Practice (partial: the eight outlined lessons)

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.5.0.**

This package is an eight-lesson partial version of layer 2 of the wet-lab practice track. **All eight outlined cell-culture topics are written: aseptic technique; media, supplements and reagent preparation; passaging adherent and suspension cells; counting cells and viability; cryopreservation and thawing; contamination, mycoplasma and authentication; human pluripotent stem-cell maintenance; and records and traceability.** The [scope crosswalk](scope-crosswalk.md) lists each status. Enrolling gives eight lessons, their practice and seven linked bench simulations, not a cell-culture course. Lesson 8 intentionally has no simulator. All eight outlined lessons now exist, but the package remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only, has no project, and establishes no competency, clinical authorization or physical validation.

The package was written with substantial AI assistance. It has not been reviewed by a laboratory professional or any certifying body, confers no credit, competency record or qualification, and does not replace the training and supervision your institution requires before you handle cells.

## Prerequisites

Laboratory Measurement and Solution Preparation, Laboratory Safety and Biosafety, and Foundations of Cell and Molecular Biology are recommended. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Perform an aseptic medium change in a Class II cabinet using pre-work checks, clean-to-dirty layout and sterile-to-sterile technique.
- Interpret contamination evidence from visible appearance, sterility incubation, filtration and dedicated mycoplasma testing, and evaluate containment and antibiotic decisions.
- Estimate viable-cell concentration and viability from hemocytometer counts, accounting for sampling, dilution, chamber geometry and a consistent boundary rule.
- Judge the reliability and meaning of a manual count: counting uncertainty, systematic preparation errors and the limits of dye-exclusion viability.
- Prepare a traceable supplemented-medium batch by calculating stock volumes, evaluating medium formulation, component suitability and QC evidence, and specifying filtration, filter-integrity checks, sterility incubation, dedicated mollicute testing, labelling, storage and release records.
- Plan and document an adherent passage to a specified viable-cell target density, contrast it with suspension passaging by dilution, and interpret split ratios and population doubling level.
- Plan and document a synthetic cell freeze–storage–thaw workflow, calculate medium, aliquot, DMSO and viable-recovery outcomes, and evaluate storage, quarantine and bank-tier traceability.
- Diagnose synthetic contamination and identity scenarios by integrating visible clues, dedicated mycoplasma evidence, STR authentication, quarantine, disposition, source tracing and incident handoff.
- Plan and document a synthetic feeder-free hPSC maintenance loop using morphology, feeding, density, clump-passaging, QC, consent and traceability evidence without implying physical-work authorization.
- Reconstruct an auditable synthetic cell-bank, passage and lot-use history, then defend a bounded contamination lead and quality-system handoff from joined records.

## Current limitations

The aseptic lesson is taught in text and practised on a synthetic medium change; the simulation cannot train hand skill, physical cabinet ergonomics, real organism identification or institutional contamination response. Its T-75 volume, clean-to-dirty zones, one-bottle rule, hidden-event probabilities, visible-growth threshold and simplified mycoplasma assay are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. FDA process-simulation incubation rules are adapted for sterility-control teaching and do not establish regulatory compliance.

The counting lesson is practised on synthetic, non-human fields; the simulation cannot train focusing, recognising cells and debris, pipetting or loading a real chamber. The top/left boundary convention, loading steps, the simulation's twofold dilution, its 20–80-cell field range and its numerical tolerances are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. Automated counters and fluorescent viability assays are named only.

The media lesson uses a synthetic 500 mL batch, pH 7.10–7.30 and 280–310 mOsm/kg windows, 98% recovery, a 10 mL control, a 14-day expiry and hidden events. These, its simplified assays and volume-weighted osmolality approximation are simulation choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria or physical validation. Real formulations, filter-integrity methods, storage and expiry follow product and institutional procedures.

Lesson 3 covers suspension passaging conceptually but simulates only an adherent T-75 target-density workflow. Its confluence window and refusal scenario, exact flask volumes, dissociation-time bound, viability penalties, target density, PDL convention and numerical tolerances are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. The incubation action records conditions but predicts no attachment, growth, phenotype, sterility or future viability. Neither the reading nor the simulation establishes physical technique, competency or institutional authorisation.

Lesson 5 covers one synthetic DMSO slow-freeze/vapour-storage pathway. Its formulation, densities, cold-dropwise implementation, temperatures, timing bounds, hidden recovery values, DMSO-removal efficiency, viability multipliers and tolerances are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. Non-permeating agents are conceptual, and vitrification, full QC release and complete bank SOPs are omitted. Neither reading nor simulation establishes physical technique, competency, institutional authorisation or future biological outcomes.

Lesson 6 uses synthetic organisms, visible panels, assay outputs, five independently assigned flask scenarios, four-locus STR profiles and source trails. Its classroom shared-call percentage is not the Tanabe or Masters algorithm or an ASN-0002 threshold. Binary control-valid tests do not model diagnostic performance, non-culturable strains, inhibition/carry-over, mixtures or stain subjectivity. No physical manipulation, species identification, disinfection, rescue efficacy or competency is established.

Lesson 7 is a synthetic hPSC maintenance decision loop only. It does not authorize or certify hPSC work, establish physical technique or competency, replace institutional biosafety/ethics review or predict real biological outcomes. Its density windows, feeding/matrix penalties, clump counts, QC interval and categorical results are teaching choices, not laboratory acceptance criteria. Public current-observation panels expose only the evidence needed for each score; unrequested assays return `not_requested`, and no hidden branch, future panel, donor identity or genomic profile is exposed.

Lesson 8 is a synthetic records-and-traceability lesson, not a bench activity. Its bank, vessel, lot and observation identifiers are original teaching data. A common lot generated by a join is an investigation lead, not causal proof. ALCOA is applied only at an introductory record level; incidents/deviations are outline-only handoffs. Detailed document control, audit trails, investigations, corrective and preventive action, GLP and CLIA remain in planned layer-4 `lab-quality-systems` and institutional procedures. The lesson intentionally has no protocol or model and establishes no physical skill, regulatory compliance or competency. Of the 33 numerical answers in the package, 0 require units, 17 check significant figures and 0 check dimensions.
