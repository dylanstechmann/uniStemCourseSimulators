# General Chemistry I/II scope crosswalk

Originally checked 2026-10-11 against General Chemistry I 0.4.8 and General Chemistry II 0.4.7 packages (Unit III row updated for General Chemistry II 0.4.8 on 2026-10-13) and MIT OpenCourseWare **5.111SC Principles of Chemical Science**, Fall 2014. MIT presents one undergraduate sequence in five units: [The Atom](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/unit-i-the-atom/), [Chemical Bonding & Structure](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/unit-ii-chemical-bonding-structure/), [Thermodynamics & Chemical Equilibrium](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/unit-iii-thermodynamics-chemical-equilibrium/), [Transition Metals & Oxidation-Reduction](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/unit-iv-transition-metals-oxidation-reduction-reactions/), and [Chemical Kinetics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/unit-v-chemical-kinetics/). The [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/syllabus/) says the freshman-level course expects one year of high-school chemistry and estimates about 150 hours.

The local packages form a coordinated sequence, not replicas of two MIT courses. General Chemistry I proposes ten weeks and General Chemistry II eleven; neither has measured workload, qualified review or institutional equivalency. The official syllabus and five units were used as link-only scope references; no teaching text, problems, images or data were copied.

## Sequence handoff

General Chemistry I is the local foundation for atoms, bonding, amount/solution accounting, gases, thermochemistry and general equilibrium. Its week-12 buffer lesson and week-13 calibration lab are explicitly introductory bridges. General Chemistry II assumes those foundations, then develops acid-base/speciation systems, free-energy direction, kinetics, electrochemistry and coordination chemistry. When adding material, deepen a stated outcome within that sequence or identify a concrete uncovered topic; do not add another generic buffer, equilibrium or molecular-shape primer.

The comparison is about topic organization and stated scope. It does not establish equal instructional depth, equivalent hours, credit, transferability or affiliation. Both local courses remain partial, formative-only and unreviewed.

## Original electronic and evidence extension (0.6.0)

Chemistry I now develops normalized hydrogenic radial probability and a two-function overlapping-basis energy calculation alongside its existing MO/hybridization lessons. Four reports connect these mechanisms with distinct chemical amount, calibration, equilibrium and mechanical-energy ledgers. Existing paired primers remain together while new blocks extend the proposal from ten to fourteen weeks. This updates the local schedule, not the one-comparator/two-local-packages boundary. Many-electron theory, measured molecular energy surfaces and real laboratory work remain incomplete. No source asset is imported.

## Second-comparator pass (0.7.0)

A package-level [scope crosswalk](../content/courses/general-chemistry-1/scope-crosswalk.md) now compares General Chemistry I with UC Irvine Chem 1A, 1B and 1C as well as MIT 5.111SC. It found two strands both comparators teach and the package lacked: the photoelectric effect, de Broglie wavelength, uncertainty principle and particle in a box, and the exceptions to the octet rule. Two original readings now close them. The Schrödinger equation is still named rather than solved, and hypervalent d-orbital hybrid pictures remain excluded. Entropy with the second and third laws is taught by both comparators and is recorded for General Chemistry II.
