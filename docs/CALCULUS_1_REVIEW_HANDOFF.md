This handoff targets Calculus I: Differential and Integral Calculus, package version 0.6.0. The [review snapshot](CALCULUS_1_REVIEW_SNAPSHOT.md) identifies the exact public files. The package remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only; no reviewer or approval is recorded.

- Review the proposed 14-week sequence, prerequisites, objective/outcome associations and mathematical level.
- Independently check the new MVT hypotheses, Rolle and uniqueness examples, finite-change bound, and representative prior items for limits, differentiation, optimization, integration and numerical error.
- Compare the week-4 inverse/exponential/logarithm reading with MIT OCW 18.01SC Unit 1 Part B; recalculate the inverse slopes, logarithmic, exponential and inverse-trigonometric derivatives, the logarithmic-differentiation results, the (1 + 1/n)ⁿ values and the pH sensitivity estimates, and confirm the branch and domain statements.
- Check the week-12 project guide and its rehearsal: the restated model and tasks against the draft, the worked one-term example, the rehearsal signal's CSV values, derivative signs, Newton steps and uniqueness argument, exact and Simpson integrals and blank sensitivity, and the wording of the five milestone self-checks. Confirm that the guide and rehearsal do not reveal the project's own results.
- Check the washout lab's dataset link, which now resolves to the package-pinned CSV, and that the project's signal table is served as a learner resource.
- Check the inactive portfolio's derivative signs, Newton iteration and root uniqueness, antiderivative, Simpson weights/error, units and blank-sensitivity result.
- Assess whether the two-component synthetic signal clearly distinguishes exact model arithmetic from data-based model selection and interpretation.
- Review sequencing, notation, accessible alternatives, manual accessibility and likely weekly workload. The proposed rubric is unweighted and does not grade prose.

## Author-side evidence and its limits

Historical 0.4.6 evidence: the focused Calculus I suite reported 82 passing tests, including recalculations for Simpson's rule, the exact integral, Newton steps and background sensitivity. The suite was not run for 0.4.7. Version 0.4.8 corrected the syllabus only. For 0.4.9 the Calculus I recalculation file recomputes all 75 numeric keys. The eight new keys use bisection-solved inverse values instead of 1/f′, centred numerical derivatives instead of the derivative formulas, and repeated multiplication for (1 + 1/n)ⁿ. A further test checks the values stated in the reading. For 0.5.0, a dedicated test recomputes every rehearsal key with independent numerics (finite differences for the derivatives, bisection for the root, a fine Simpson sum for the exact integral), checks the guide's worked example and the CSV values against their formulas, and confirms that neither the guide nor the rehearsal states the project's own results. The full-suite result is in the validation report. These author-side checks do not establish independent mathematical review, accessible learner usability, semester workload, student learning or university equivalency. Graded homework, midterm, final and a laboratory sequence remain absent; the portfolio draft is inactive, and its optional learner guide is ungraded practice.
