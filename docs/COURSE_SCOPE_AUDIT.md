# Repository-wide course scope audit

Checked 2026-10-11. This is an AI-assisted first-pass curriculum audit of all 25 authored packages, using their manifests, syllabi, module headings, selected readings and targeted searches alongside official MIT OpenCourseWare syllabi and calendars. It is not independent scientific review, a question-by-question correctness audit, or evidence of university equivalence. A topic mentioned in a short overview is not necessarily developed instruction. Proposed weeks and content counts do not measure learner workload.

Current inventory: **25 partial courses, 446 lessons, 2,906 formative questions, 1,562 retrieval cards and 25 self-assessed cases**. The 37 additional catalog topics are planning nodes. Course versions below identify the inspected packages; update an affected row with its next substantive change rather than regenerating the entire audit after every edit.

## Redundancy and scope decisions

Retain brief prerequisite reminders and genuinely different applications. Consolidate exposition when the same growth/washout, oxygen-limit, sensor-calibration, independent-unit, control-selection or uncertainty explanation repeats without teaching a new skill. Link the Statistics/Programming foundations and keep each course's domain-specific reasoning. Preserve stable lesson IDs, learner history and archived assessment/review evidence when changing schedules or navigation.

Several packages combine university subjects, while Calculus I/II and General Chemistry I/II split a comparator's sequence. Define local boundaries explicitly; do not fill every omitted MIT topic into every package. Graduate and advanced-seminar comparators are selective, not mandatory depth targets for introductory courses. New catalog courses are lower priority than the demonstrated foundational gaps.
