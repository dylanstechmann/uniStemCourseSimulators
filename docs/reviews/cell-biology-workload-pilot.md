# Cell Biology workload pilot protocol

**Status:** prospective protocol only. No learners have been recruited, no time data have been collected, and this document is not workload evidence. It is scoped to the proposed 14-week Foundations of Cell and Molecular Biology course at version 0.61.0. The course and its assessment candidates remain partial, inactive, and unreviewed.

**Public package pin:** archive SHA-256 `63ce2392d5586830e414c4e214af42de73adef03a9f1276dbbd58024a4a9b50a`; manifest SHA-256 `75449a3d7439567f29afd56bb88e81e21340d7bd68eaa5d469dc4d1bf24de173`. These hashes identify public package files only; they do not indicate approval. See the [versioned 0.61.0 review snapshot](../CELL_BIOLOGY_REVIEW_SNAPSHOT-0.61.0.md). This protocol supersedes the 0.60.0 pilot protocol; earlier version-bound artifacts remain historical.

## Purpose and limits

Measure the time learners need for activities in an owner-approved, frozen plan for the proposed 14-week course. Include assigned instruction and required work; report optional practice separately. Include homework, labs, exams, and project work only if each has passed review and is assigned in that frozen plan. The pilot estimates time for the tested version and sample; it does not establish learning effectiveness, academic credit equivalence, or the workload of another version or learner population.

The current package has no enrolled graded assignments, accepted assessment schedule, weekly time targets, or selected credit/contact model. Its inactive candidates cannot be treated as assigned work. Do not start this pilot until an owner has frozen the learner-facing course and chosen the intended course format and workload band for each week. If those targets are not set, the study can describe observed time but cannot judge whether that time is appropriate.

## Conditions before recruitment

1. Freeze the exact course version, all required and optional activities, reading pages, lab interactions, candidate forms, due dates, and project milestones. Record the version and content hashes in the study report. Any material change during the pilot ends that cohort's comparability; start a new cohort for the changed version.
2. Obtain independent subject-matter, assessment, and accessibility review of the materials that participants will use. Decide which assessments are required and which are optional. The current inactive drafts do not satisfy this condition.
3. Have the responsible institution determine what ethics and privacy review is required before inviting participants. Do not recruit or collect data until that determination and the participant information/consent process are in place.
4. Before seeing results, record the expected student time range for each week, whether scheduled contact time is included, the intended course format, and the rule for investigating an overlong week. Base those choices on the course's actual institutional context; this protocol does not assign credit hours or invent a universal workload target.
5. Use a coordinator who does not grade participants. Participation and withdrawal must have no effect on course access, grades, or recommendations. Do not use current students when the instructor can affect their grades unless the institution-approved process protects that choice.

## Participants and duration

Invite about 15 adult volunteers and aim for at least 12 who complete all 14 weekly blocks. Recruit across the preparation levels the course is intended to serve, including learners with little recent biology study and learners with relevant prior study. This is a descriptive pilot, not a powered comparison; report the actual sample and do not generalize beyond it. If fewer than 12 participants complete all weeks, report the results as incomplete pilot evidence and do not describe the full-course workload as measured.

Run the pilot for the full 14-week sequence using the frozen learner experience. A shortened trial may identify confusing or unusually long activities, but it cannot stand in for a semester workload estimate. Give all participants the same stated pacing and due-date expectations. Record the date ranges for each week in the aggregate study record, not in the learner log.

## What to record

For every activity, ask participants to enter the time log immediately after working. Record active minutes, excluding unrelated interruptions and breaks. Include time spent reading instructions, using required course tools, completing the work, checking feedback, and retrying when retries are part of the stated course policy. Record interruptions separately when they materially lengthen a session. Do not collect precise start/end timestamps, keystrokes, screen recordings, or browsing histories.

Give each participant a random study code. Keep the code-to-identity list separately with the coordinator and delete it on the schedule approved for the study. The log must not contain names, email addresses, student IDs, diagnoses, or free-text comments that could identify a person. The supplied blank CSV header is in [`cell-biology-workload-log-template-0.61.0.csv`](cell-biology-workload-log-template-0.61.0.csv). Use one row per participant, course activity, and attempt/session; never enter a zero to represent missing time.

Log the following separately:

- required scheduled instruction or contact time, if any;
- required readings and lesson activities;
- required formative practice, homework, labs, exams, and project milestones in the frozen plan;
- optional practice, retrieval cards, retries beyond the requirement, and enrichment work;
- active minutes, interruption minutes, completion state, and whether a task was resumed;
- a small set of non-identifying barrier codes (content unclear, technical difficulty, access barrier, outside interruption, or other/prefer not to say).

Do not ask participants to disclose why they use an accommodation or assistive technology. Ask separately and optionally whether any course activity was difficult to access, and allow a private route to report an access issue to the coordinator. Report accessibility findings in a way that does not expose a small group or an individual.

Use `record_status=session` for an observed work session, `not_attempted` when a participant confirms that an activity was not started, and `missing` when the status is unknown. Leave minutes blank for `not_attempted` and `missing`; zero is reserved for an explicitly observed zero-minute activity. Use `completed=yes`, `partial`, `no`, or `na`. Use `resumed=yes/no`; use a numeric session number starting at 1. Allowed barrier codes are `none`, `content_unclear`, `technical`, `access`, `interruption`, and `other_or_prefer_not_to_say`. A blank barrier code means it was not asked; it does not mean `none`.

At each week's close, send a short neutral check-in asking whether all planned tasks were logged, whether any required task was skipped because of time, and whether the week felt lighter or heavier than expected. A skipped task is not zero minutes. Preserve its status and reason category separately from time totals.

## Analysis and review rules

Before collection, define the expected time band for each week and whether it includes contact time. After collection:

- calculate each participant's total required and optional minutes separately for each week;
- report the number of participants with complete data for every estimate, the median and interquartile range, and the 75th percentile for each week and each major activity type;
- show contact time separately and include it in the weekly total only if the predeclared target includes it;
- report completion, skipped-task, resumed-session, and missing-log counts; do not impute missing entries as zero;
- summarize preparation groups descriptively only when cell sizes protect privacy; do not make significance or subgroup claims from this small sample;
- compare each weekly distribution with the predeclared band, and investigate any week where the 75th percentile exceeds its upper bound, where required work is skipped because of time, or where repeated access barriers appear;
- document the course-owner decision for each flagged week: revise the task, change pacing, adjust the target with a justified reason, or retain it with an explicit explanation. A decision made after results are visible must be labeled post hoc.

These review flags trigger discussion; they do not automatically pass or fail the course. If a week or assessment changes, repeat measurement for that material with a new version-bound cohort before claiming that its workload is understood. No single pilot result establishes a universally appropriate amount of work.

## Reporting and data handling

Keep raw logs and any identity key outside Git in an access-controlled location with a documented deletion date. Publish only aggregate results and suppress small cells that could identify a participant. The report should record the course/content version, activity inventory, target bands set before collection, recruitment and completion counts, missingness, weekly and activity-level distributions, access barriers, changes made, repeat measurements, and limitations. Do not publish participant-level rows or claim independent review from the author's analysis.

The current state is **not collected**. After the prerequisites are met, link the dated aggregate report here and update the course progress record. Until then, workload remains an open completion gate.
