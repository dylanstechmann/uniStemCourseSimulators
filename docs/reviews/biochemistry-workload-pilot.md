## Purpose and limits

Estimate the time learners need for a frozen, owner-approved 14-week learner plan. Report required instruction and work separately from optional practice. The pilot estimates time for the tested version and sample. It does not establish learning effectiveness, academic credit equivalence, or workload for another version or learner population.

The current package has no graded assignments, accepted list of required activities, due dates, weekly time targets, or selected credit/contact model. All practice is open and ungraded. Since version 0.5.0, eight open homework practice sets, midterm and cumulative final review practice and an optional protein-state project with milestone self-checks are available; the owner may designate any of them as required or optional for a pilot. The separate inactive homework, exam and project candidates cannot be counted as assigned work. Do not start recruitment until the course owner freezes the learner-facing plan and identifies which activities are required. If weekly target ranges and the contact-time rule are not set in advance, the study can describe observed time but cannot judge whether that time is appropriate.

## Conditions before recruitment

1. Freeze the exact learner-facing version, required and optional activity inventory, readings, virtual labs, due dates and any approved assessment forms. Record the course version and package/archive hashes. A material change during the pilot ends that cohort's comparability; begin a new version-bound cohort for changed content.
2. Obtain qualified biochemical, assessment, accessibility and fairness review of the materials participants will use. Decide which activities are required. The inactive homework, exam and project drafts do not satisfy this condition and must not be assigned by this protocol.
3. Ask the responsible institution what ethics and privacy review applies. Do not recruit or collect data until that determination and the participant-information and consent process are complete.
4. Before seeing results, record the expected time range for each week, whether scheduled contact time is included, the intended course format and the rule for investigating an overlong week. The protocol does not assign credit hours or invent a universal workload target.
5. Use a coordinator who does not grade participants. Participation and withdrawal must not affect course access, grades or recommendations. Do not recruit current students whose grades the instructor controls unless the institution-approved process protects that choice.

## Participants, logging and analysis

Invite approximately 15 adult volunteers and aim for at least 12 who complete all 14 weekly blocks. Recruit across the preparation levels the course is intended to serve. This descriptive pilot is not a powered comparison; report the actual sample and do not generalize beyond it. Fewer than 12 complete participants means the result is an incomplete pilot, not a measured full-course workload estimate. Run all 14 weeks with the same pacing and learner experience; a shortened trial can find confusing or long tasks but cannot stand in for semester workload.

Use one row per participant, course activity and work session in [`biochemistry-workload-log-template.csv`](biochemistry-workload-log-template.csv). Record active minutes after each session, including reading instructions, using required tools, completing work, checking feedback and retries that are part of the stated learner policy. Exclude unrelated breaks and interruptions; record material interruption minutes separately. Do not collect precise start/end times, keystrokes, screen recordings or browsing histories.

Assign each participant a random study code. Keep any identity key separately with the coordinator and delete it on the institution-approved schedule. The log must contain no name, email, student ID, diagnosis or identifying free-text comment. Do not ask why a participant uses an accommodation or assistive technology. Ask separately and optionally whether an activity was difficult to access, with a private route to the coordinator; suppress small groups in any report.

Use `record_status=session` for an observed session, `not_attempted` when a participant confirms an activity was not started, and `missing` when status is unknown. Leave minutes blank for `not_attempted` and `missing`; zero is reserved for an explicitly observed zero-minute session. Use `completed=yes`, `partial`, `no` or `na`; `resumed=yes/no`; and numeric session numbers starting at 1. Allowed barrier codes are `none`, `content_unclear`, `technical`, `access`, `interruption` and `other_or_prefer_not_to_say`. A blank barrier means it was not asked, not that the participant reported no barrier.

At each week's close, ask neutrally whether all planned work was logged, whether a required task was skipped for time, and whether the week felt lighter or heavier than expected. A skipped task is not zero minutes. Preserve completion and missingness separately from time totals.

Before collection, define target ranges and the contact-time rule. After collection:

- calculate each participant's required and optional minutes separately for each week;
- report participant counts, medians, interquartile ranges and 75th percentiles by week and major activity type, with the number of complete observations for every estimate;
- show contact time separately and include it in weekly totals only if the predeclared target includes it;
- report completion, skipped-task, resumed-session and missing-log counts; do not impute missing time as zero;
- describe preparation groups only when cell sizes protect privacy and avoid significance claims from this small sample;
- compare each weekly distribution with its predeclared band, investigating weeks where the 75th percentile exceeds the upper bound, required work is skipped for time, or repeated access barriers appear;
- record the owner decision for each flagged week: revise work, change pacing, adjust the target for a justified reason, or retain it with an explanation. Label decisions made after results are visible as post hoc.

These flags prompt review; they do not automatically pass or fail the course. If a week or assessment changes, repeat measurement for the changed material with a new version-bound cohort before claiming its workload is understood.

Keep raw logs and any identity key outside Git in access-controlled storage with a documented deletion date. Publish only aggregate results. The report should record course version and hashes, frozen activity inventory, predeclared target bands, recruitment/completion counts, missingness, weekly and activity-level distributions, access barriers, changes, repeat measurements and limitations. Do not publish participant rows or describe the author's analysis as independent review.
